## Why

Today a host save stores each v6 window's canvas as a PNG (`pictures/win-N.png`). Two costs:

1. **A restored window cannot be replayed.** SQ-0567 recolours the screen on a palette change (Blorb §11.3) by clearing each canvas and replaying its display list; a restored window's list is empty, so SQ-0587 had to mark those windows `unreplayable`. Correct, but it means restored art keeps its SAVED colours until the game next redraws it wholesale.
2. **Pixels mask our own bugs.** A snapshot restores the screen even when our op recording is incomplete — the failure only surfaces later, indirectly, as it did in SQ-0587 (art vanishing one move after a restore). Rebuilding from ops makes a recording gap show up AT RESTORE, where it is obvious and attributable.

Storing what the story *did*, rather than what the screen *looked like*, is also the smaller artifact: Arthur's two canvases are ~34 KB of PNG per save; its op list is a handful of draw/erase records.

## Scope

- **Persist the display list.** `display_ops: HashMap<u8, Vec<V6Op>>` already exists and is maintained (`record_op`), with a cap (`V6_OPS_CAP`) and an `unreplayable` escape hatch. Add a serde DTO beside the existing ones in `archive.rs` (`ScreenDto`/`V6WindowsDto` are the pattern) and a new archive entry.
- **Persist the Current Palette.** `PictSource::current_plte` + `palette_gen` (`graphics.rs`) are host state and are archived NOWHERE today. A replay without them decodes adaptive pictures under the wrong palette — §11.3 calls an adaptive picture drawn before any non-adaptive one undefined. This is required for the rest to be correct, and is arguably a latent bug already.
- **Rebuild on restore** by replaying under the restored palette, instead of `load_pictures_png`.
- **Keep a PNG fallback per window**, not as the default: see the self-check below.

## The self-check that makes this safe

At SAVE time, replay each window's ops into a scratch canvas and compare against the live canvas.

- Equal → store ops only.
- Differ → store that window's PNG as well, and record a diagnostic naming the window.

The archive stays correct by construction, while a recording gap becomes a visible, attributable diagnostic instead of a silent divergence. That is the "identify issues earlier" property, without shipping a regression to get it.

## Open questions

- `V6_OPS_CAP`: is it large enough for real games? Measure list lengths across Arthur, Zork Zero, Shogun, Journey and advent over a play session. Arthur resets its list on whole-canvas ops (screen swaps), which is what keeps it short — confirm the others do too.
- A window `unreplayable` at save time (cap overflow) falls back to its PNG. Fine, but it should be counted, not silent.
- Old archives carry PNGs and no ops: `#[serde(default)]` keeps them loading, and they take the fallback path. Pre-release rules allow breaking the format, but there is no reason to.

## Acceptance

- `zork0_v6_persistence`'s byte-identical composite assertion still holds with canvases rebuilt from ops rather than loaded.
- `v6_restore_palette_replay` (SQ-0587) strengthens from "the restored art SURVIVES a palette change" to "the restored art is RECOLOURED by it", which is only possible once restored windows are replayable.
- A new case: corrupt/blank one window's ops, save, restore, and assert the self-check caught it and fell back rather than restoring a blank window.
