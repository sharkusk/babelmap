## The claim

`zvm::interpreter`'s IBM PC row (interpreter 6) declines `default_colours`. The
evidence says it should be **bg 6 BLUE / fg 9 WHITE**.

Note what that does NOT require: §8.3.1 numbers blue 6 and white 9 resolve through
the palette and the user's theme, so there is no RGB to source and no
"which blue" question. This would be **better** evidenced than SQ-0873's period
look, not worse — that field had to record what one emulator drew.

## The evidence, and it is two independent kinds agreeing

**(a) DOS captures**, supplied by the user from walkthrough video — three frames,
all reporting the IBM interpreter in their own banners:

- *Shogun* r322 / Pix 21 / 890706, "IBM Interpreter version 6.68", at the main
  menu: **white on blue**, gold ornament flanks on black strips, black outside them.
- *Arthur* r74, mid-game (Churchyard, St Anne's Day, Compline): **white on blue**,
  EGA-dithered art.
- *Zork Zero*, Banquet Hall: **black on white**, orange banner ribbons.

The user also reports that in the video **Zork Zero's boot sequence is blue/white
until the game starts** — i.e. before it issues any colour of its own. That is the
observation that turns a coincidence into a default.

**(b) The screen-op trace**, run independently through
`Engine::set_trace_screen` / `take_screen_trace`, listing every DISTINCT
`@set_colour` each game issues:

    arthur   941 ops / 30 taps   fg=0,bg=0  ·  fg=default,bg=default   → names NOTHING
    journey  532 ops /  6 taps   fg=0,bg=0                             → names NOTHING
    shogun   183 ops /  6 taps   fg=0,bg=0 (menu, window 2)
                                 fg=std2, bg=std9 (window 1, in play)
    zork0     18 ops /  2 taps   fg=std2, bg=std9

`fg=0`/`bg=0` and `default` are the no-change sentinels — those games never name a
colour at all.

**Cross the two and every frame is explained by one rule:** the interpreter's
default is blue/white; a game that names nothing keeps it (Arthur, Journey,
Shogun's menu); a game that names black-on-white gets that (Zork Zero, Shogun in
play).

**A FALSIFIABLE PREDICTION falls out and should be checked:** Shogun's screen must
**flip from blue at the menu to white once play starts**, because that is exactly
where its `set_colour(std2, std9)` lands. If a video shows the gameplay screen
still blue, this model is wrong.

**A DISCARDED DATA POINT, recorded so it is not rediscovered:** an earlier Arthur
frame from a different walkthrough showed a GREY ground and appeared to refute
this outright — Arthur names no colour, and its art canvas is only 40% opaque
(the knotwork border; `rgba(32,32,96,255)` and friends), so the grey was neither
declared nor drawn. The user judged that video not to be true DOS emulation and
found one that included the boot; it is blue. **A walkthrough recording is not a
machine capture** — it may be a modern interpreter reporting itself as an IBM.

## FIXTURES ARE MISSING and the claim should not land without them

The three frames exist only in the conversation. Pasted images do not reach the
filesystem, so they could not be committed at the time. **The user will add them
to `machine-screenshots/`.** Until then this quest rests on testimony, which is
below the standard the rest of the machine table meets.

They also close the gap SQ-0873 left open: interpreter 6 had no capture at all,
which is why its period look is declined.

## THE OBSTACLE, and it is the whole of the work

`InterpreterProfile::IbmPc` is not only "an IBM PC". `InterpreterProfile::resolve`
returns it when **nothing named a machine at all** — no medium, no configured
number — which is every modern Inform story anyone opens. `default_colours()`
cannot tell "the player asked for interpreter 6" from "there was nothing to ask",
because both are the same variant.

So `default_colours: Some((6, 9))` on that row paints **Counterfeit Monkey blue**.

That is also precisely why the row declines today, and the existing doc says so in
different words: *"an IBM PC in a terminal is the player's terminal, so 'default'
should mean what the player actually sees."* That reasoning is sound for the
FALLBACK and wrong for the MACHINE, and the enum currently cannot hold both.

Shapes worth weighing (none agreed):

1. **Split the variant** — an explicit `IbmPc` distinct from a `Terminal`/`Unnamed`
   fallback. Honest, and it touches `resolve`, every match arm, and the agreement
   test in `interpreter_profile`.
2. **Keep `default_colours` declining and put the pair somewhere presentation-only**,
   the way SQ-0873 separated the period look from the sourced pair. But this pair
   is a fact a v5+ story READS in `$2C`/`$2D`, so hiding it in presentation
   misrepresents it — the very confusion SQ-0873's first note forbids.
3. **Gate on how the profile was reached** — carry provenance (asked-for vs
   inferred vs fallback) alongside the profile. Generalises past this one row.

## Relationship to SQ-0927

This would very likely DISSOLVE SQ-0927. That quest is about a v6 frame with no
machine pair giving two different answers to "no colour named" — chrome text cells
take the `chrome` role's black while the pane takes no ground at all. Give
interpreter 6 a pair and `v6_machine_page` fills both, and the split disappears on
exactly the frames where it is visible. Decide this one first; SQ-0927 may need
nothing but a test.

## THE PREDICTION IN THE NOTE ABOVE WAS WRONG, AND ITS FAILURE IMPROVES THE MODEL

That note predicted Shogun's screen would flip from blue at the menu to white once
play started, on the strength of its `@set_colour(fg=std2, bg=std9, window=1)`.
The user reports every walkthrough online shows **DOS blue/white staying
throughout gameplay**. The prediction is falsified.

The error: a `set_colour` names a WINDOW's pair, not the screen's. Reading
`window=1` as a screen-wide override was the mistake, and measuring which windows
each game actually colours settles it.

    shogun   (gameplay)  1 of 5 windows named:  Grid (46,0 548x32)   bg=9 fg=2
    zork0                3 of 5 windows named:  Buffer (86,78 468x320)
                                                Grid   (0,0  640x78)
                                                Grid   (0,0  640x400)   <-- the SCREEN
    arthur / journey     none

Shogun colours a **548x32 status strip** — the black-on-white location/score bar
`fill_explicit_bg_rows` already documents (SQ-0519) — and leaves the ground
untouched. Zork Zero is the only game here that colours a window the size of the
whole screen, which is why it, and only it, is white.

## The model, now with no exceptions

| game | windows named | covers 640x400 | screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arthur | none | — | blue |
| Journey | none | — | blue |
| Shogun | one 548x32 strip | no | **blue**, white status bar |
| Zork Zero | three, incl. 640x400 | **yes** | white |

Four games, four captures, one rule: **the IBM interpreter's default page is blue 6
under white 9, and games layer per-window pairs on top of it.** That is precisely
what `default_colours` is defined to be — ZMSD §8.3.3's default page and ink, the
ground every window that names nothing is read on — so the claim and the field now
mean the same thing, which the earlier framing did not quite.

The evidence is now stronger than when this quest was filed: the one apparent
counterexample turned out to confirm the rule once measured properly, and it did so
by a mechanism (window size) that nothing in the original reasoning had to assume.

**Lesson worth keeping past this quest:** a falsifiable prediction earned its keep
here by being wrong. It forced the per-window/per-screen distinction into the open,
which the "games override the default" phrasing had been quietly blurring — and
which matters for any future machine-pair work, because `v6_machine_page` lays a
pair under EVERY window and a per-window `set_colour` must still win over it.

## THE USER'S RULING, which settles the obstacle this quest was stuck on

> "System colors (including our newly discovered IBM PC colors) should ONLY apply
> if running the game from original media."
> "and maybe add an arg that allows a user to use system colors if they want"

**The MEDIUM is the licence.** That resolves the problem cleanly and without
splitting the enum: `IbmPc` reached as a fallback (a bare `.z6`, a modern `.gblorb`,
anything with no medium) supplies no pair and the player's terminal governs, exactly
as today. `IbmPc` reached because a DOS floppy or a `.ima` was mounted supplies
blue 6 under white 9. Counterfeit Monkey never turns blue.

Note what this generalises to: it is not an IBM PC rule at all. **Every** machine's
pair becomes conditional on the medium — the Amiga's 12/9, the Macintosh's 9/2, the
Apple's 2/9. Today those are reachable by `interpreter_number` alone, and this rule
takes that away by default, which is a **change to a documented contract**.
`docs/features/interpreter.md` currently promises:

> "Setting `interpreter_number` yourself names the machine outright and outranks
> both, so `interpreter_number = 4` gets you the whole Amiga rather than just the
> byte — which is the point: a number that changed what games did without changing
> the machine it implied was never a useful thing to be able to set."

The opt-in arg is what preserves that: `--interpreter 4` alone would advertise the
byte and leave colours to the terminal; `--interpreter 4` **plus** the opt-in gets
the Amiga's pair on a bare file. Worth naming explicitly in the docs as a change of
promise rather than letting it drift.

Shape to settle when this is implemented (nothing agreed yet):

- Where the condition lives. `InterpreterProfile::resolve` currently discards HOW it
  reached its answer. Carrying that provenance — asked-for / from-medium / fallback
  — is the general form, and `default_colours()` becomes a question about the launch
  rather than about the enum.
- The arg's spelling and home: a CLI flag, a `config.toml` key, or both. It is a
  per-launch presentation choice like `--no-game-colours`, so probably both, with a
  one-run pin (SQ-0807) so it cannot bleed into the global file.
- **`$2C`/`$2D` is READ BY THE STORY.** Whatever gates this gates a fact, not a
  paint: a v5+ game asking for the default pair must get the same answer the screen
  shows. That is SQ-0873's distinction and it must not blur here either.

## Regression case to write BEFORE the change, not after

A per-window `set_colour` must keep winning over a machine pair. `v6_machine_page`
lays the pair under EVERY window, and Shogun is the specimen: booted from media its
ground must go blue **while its 548x32 status strip stays white-on-black**, because
that strip is the one window it colours. Get that wrong and the pair silently eats a
colour the game did name — the opposite of what this quest is for.
