--- note 2026-08-12T06:51:12Z ---

USER OBSERVATION 2026-08-11, after verifying all four Zork Zero renditions made selectable by SQ-0734 + SQ-0790:

> "what is interesting is that the mg1 looks like the blb version with the rose that is cut-off on the bottom (vs pic and amiga adf)"

    zork0.pic  (Amiga native)   compass rose COMPLETE
    Amiga .adf                  compass rose COMPLETE
    zork0.mg1  (MCGA)           rose CUT OFF at the bottom
    Zork0.blb  (Blorb)          rose CUT OFF at the bottom

**THIS REVIVES AN OPEN QUESTION SQ-0713 RECORDED AND COULD NOT SETTLE.** That quest noted the user seeing a complete circle on Amiga against a truncated one in the Blorb, and asked whether it was one of the five known dimension-mismatch pictures (ids 5, 6, 7, 8, 33) or a case where dimensions match and the CONTENT still differs. It could not tell, having only two sources. There are now four, and they split 2-2 along a line that suggests a completely different answer.

**LEADING HYPOTHESIS, AND IT IS NOT ABOUT THE ARTWORK: the variable may be the INTERPRETER PROFILE, not the archive.** SQ-0734 shipped the rule that *an archive's flavour selects the interpreter number unless one is set explicitly*. So:
- naming `zork0.pic` selects **Amiga**
- naming `zork0.mg1` selects **IBM PC**
- a Blorb infers nothing and takes the default, which for v6 is **IBM PC** (Frotz's rule, byte 0x1E = 6)

That partitions the four sources **exactly** along the observed line: the two that render Amiga show a complete rose, the two that render IBM PC show a truncated one. If that holds, the art is identical in all four and the truncation is a *rendering* difference between profiles — most likely a clip against a window boundary that the two profiles size differently.

This also dissolves the tension SQ-0713 could not resolve: its own measurement found the Blorb **383/388 byte-exact against the Amiga `Pic.data`**, which is very hard to reconcile with the Blorb having genuinely different rose artwork. If nothing about the art differs, that contradiction disappears.

**THE DECISIVE EXPERIMENT, and it is cheap — do this before reading any code:** cross the two variables.

    zork0.pic  with an EXPLICIT IbmPc interpreter   -> rose complete or cut?
    zork0.mg1  with an EXPLICIT Amiga interpreter   -> rose complete or cut?

An explicit interpreter setting outranks the archive-derived one (SQ-0734's precedence), so both runs are expressible today with no code change. If the rose follows the **profile**, this is a profile/clipping defect and the archives are innocent. If it follows the **archive**, the art really does differ and SQ-0713's five-mismatch list is incomplete.

IF IT IS THE PROFILE: the next question is which behaviour is correct. The Amiga one is the complete circle and is presumably what the artist drew, so the IBM PC path is probably clipping something it should not — but establish that rather than assuming, since the DOS release may genuinely have shown it clipped and faithfulness could cut either way.

BLAST RADIUS IF IT IS A CLIP: whatever clips the rose clips everything else drawn near that boundary, in every v6 game under that profile. Check the corpus rather than fixing the rose.

RELATED: SQ-0713 (native picture reading, where the observation originated), SQ-0734 (the flavour → interpreter rule that makes the profile the hidden variable here), SQ-0790 (which made the renditions comparable at matching geometry).

NOT YET SCHEDULED: this touches the render path, where a lane is currently working on side-border tiling (SQ-0698) and another on EGA/CGA palettes (SQ-0794). Dispatch when a render slot frees, to avoid two lanes in the same files.

--- note 2026-08-12T06:52:09Z ---

**HYPOTHESIS KILLED 2026-08-11 by the next data point. The profile theory in the note above is WRONG — disregard it.** User:

> "all the other formats have the full rose"

    zork0.pic  Amiga     rose COMPLETE
    Amiga .adf           rose COMPLETE
    zork0.eg1  EGA       rose COMPLETE
    zork0.cg1  CGA       rose COMPLETE
    zork0.mg1  MCGA      rose CUT OFF
    Zork0.blb  Blorb     rose CUT OFF

**`.eg1` and `.cg1` are PC flavours and select the IBM PC profile exactly as `.mg1` does.** They show the full rose. So the interpreter profile cannot be the variable, and the clean 2-2 Amiga/PC partition recorded above was a coincidence of having only four sources. The split is **MCGA + Blorb against everything else**, which puts this back to being about the artwork.

**AND IT MAKES SQ-0713's CONCLUSION HARDER, NOT EASIER.** That quest measured `Zork0.blb` as **383/388 byte-exact against the Amiga `zork0.pic`** and concluded the Blorb is an Amiga conversion — corroborated independently by every Blorb PLTE being pure 12-bit Amiga RGB (channel bytes all multiples of 0x11). Yet the Blorb's rose matches **MCGA**, not Amiga. Both cannot be simply true. Either the rose is one of the five known dimension mismatches (ids 5, 6, 7, 8, 33 — SQ-0713 explicitly asked whether the rose was among them and could not tell), or the Blorb is not the single-source conversion it appears to be.

**THE EXPERIMENT IS NOW ENTIRELY OFFLINE AND MUCH BETTER — no rendering, no profiles, no interpreter crossing.** babelmap decodes every one of these sources today: `crates/blorb/src/infocom_pics.rs` reads the Amiga Huffman flavour (SQ-0713) and the PC LZW flavour (SQ-0735), and the Blorb's PNGs are readable directly. So:

1. **Identify the compass rose's picture id.** SQ-0713 recorded ids 1000-1011 in the Blorb as "compass needles (N/S/E/W plus unlabelled diagonals)", which is the needle set rather than the rose plate; the rose itself is a separate id and needs finding.
2. **Decode that id from all five archives** and compare dimensions and pixels pairwise.
3. That answers it outright: if MCGA's stored art is genuinely shorter, the truncation is in the original DOS media and we are rendering it faithfully. If MCGA's art is complete and only *our* render truncates it, it is our defect.

Point 3 is the fork that matters, and it is worth stating plainly: **this may not be a babelmap bug at all.** Infocom shipped different art per platform, and a DOS release with a clipped rose would be historically accurate. The user's preference for the complete circle is aesthetic, not evidence of a defect — establish which before treating it as one.

WHAT THE ANSWER IS WORTH BEYOND THE ROSE: if MCGA and Blorb agree against Amiga on this picture, the provenance of `Zork0.blb` is more complicated than SQ-0713 concluded, and that bears on every claim resting on "the Blorb is the Amiga art" — including the 383/388 oracle that validated the Amiga decoder. Worth knowing whether that oracle has an asterisk.

**NO LONGER BLOCKED ON A RENDER SLOT.** The previous note deferred this to avoid colliding with the tiling and palette lanes. Since the experiment is a `crates/blorb` decode-and-compare with no render involvement, it can run alongside them.
