--- note 2026-08-19T03:07:41Z ---

The hypothesis, and why the user's ear is evidence rather than preference: Sherlock plays three heartbeats (effects 11, 12, 13) off ONE sample against three pitch files reading 68, 74 and 79. SQ-0912 concluded the Amiga plays all three identically because every Amiga `.mid` is a unison pair — note == base within each file, so the ratio is 1. The user confirms that is what babelmap does, and that the Macintosh (which does bend them) sounds right while the Amiga does not.

What makes the unison reading suspect:

1. **`base` is 68 on ALL FOUR Macintosh index files** — M8, M11, M13, M14 — while `note` varies. Those four do not share a sample (M11 and M13 point at S12; M8 and M14 do not), so `base` cannot be a property of the sample, and it is constant across effects, so it is not a property of the effect either. A constant that survives both is a **per-game reference note**, not a per-file value.

2. The Amiga `.mid` files hold the same note TWICE — (68,68), (74,74), (79,79). If the interpreter takes `note` from the file and `base` from a per-game constant of 68, the Amiga bends by 0, +6 and +11 semitones — **exactly the Macintosh's model**, and the duplicated second byte in the `.mid` is padding rather than meaning.

3. Our own disassembly note (recorded on `Pitch` in `crates/blorb/src/infocom_sound.rs`) already says the routine at 0x48da in hunk 8 of the Amiga Lurking Horror takes "one note read from the pitch buffer at the cursor and one from a **per-effect field the interpreter captured earlier**". Whether that captured field is the pitch file's own second byte or something from elsewhere is the whole question, and it was assumed rather than traced.

4. It would explain the Blorb, which currently reads as incoherent: `stories/Sherlock.blb` fits `note − 74` and is "self-inconsistent on effect 13". If the reference is per-game — 74 for Lurking Horror, 68 for Sherlock — then the Blorb's author found 74 on Lurking Horror and carried it to Sherlock, where it is the wrong constant. That turns a third rendition from noise into corroboration.

What to do: re-read the Amiga interpreter around 0x48da and find where the second note comes from — story file, interpreter constant, or the pitch buffer. If it is not the file's own second byte, the Amiga model changes and `every_amiga_pitch_file_is_a_unison_pair` plus `the_macintosh_release_bends_the_shared_heartbeat` both need revisiting, along with the `Pitch` docs, which currently present the unison as a finding.

Do NOT settle this by ear alone — but do note that machine-screenshots/ and an emulator running the Amiga release are the falsifying instrument here, per the standing rule that internal measurement cannot tell you the question was wrong. A recording of Sherlock's three heartbeats on a real Amiga would end the argument in one pass.
