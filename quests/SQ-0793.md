  SQ-0698 taught the HYBRID ring to tile side border art; raster mode was left alone, and the two modes now differ in a way worth stating. In native space Shogun's border ends at row 336 of 400 and Arthur's poles at 379, so the raster composite — which scales the whole 640x400 canvas uniformly and letterboxes it — shows an unpainted band inside the frame's own lower edge. Zork Zero is unaffected (its pillars already paint to row 400). Deliberately deferred to keep SQ-0698's blast radius to one mode's corpus. Note the divergence predates this work in the other direction: SQ-0511's flank STRETCH was hybrid-only too.

GEOMETRY NOTE for the user's direction ("raster should always be rendered at the PC resolution"): 640x400 was already the composition space and there is one scale, so no geometry change was needed. `native_extent` returns (640, 400) for all three specimens; `build_v6_raster_canvas` returns a canvas of exactly that size; the raster branch hands the whole canvas to one resize+encode (`spawn_v6_encode`). The defect was purely that the flanks were not COMPLETE before that scale. Nothing is scaled per band, so the corner agreement SQ-0698's suite asserts as a relation on the hybrid side comes for free here.

THE CHANGE: `extend_raster_flanks` in screen.rs, called from build_v6_raster_canvas between the bare graphics canvas being built and `grounds` being applied to it — so the classifier reads the same image hybrid classifies from ("opaque" is not "artwork", SQ-0500) while the pixels are cut from the chrome canvas, exactly as `flank_tiled_source` pairs them. Flank columns are those either side of the story window's declared native rect. Rows above the art come back byte-for-byte.

TEST: `the_raster_composite_extends_its_side_art_to_the_native_bottom` (v6_side_border_tiling.rs, case 8), both honor_game_colours modes. Pins native == (640,400) and canvas == native, then asserts the flank below the art is not one flat colour AND matches the extension the art itself dictates, pixel for pixel wherever that extension is opaque.
