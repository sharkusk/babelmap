## Why nothing in Rust can catch these

The messages come from libasound (reached through rodio → cpal), which writes to **file descriptor 2 directly** from C. They never pass through `std::io::stderr`, so no Rust-side hook, `set_hook`, or captured-writer trick can intercept them — they land on the terminal mid-frame and corrupt the TUI. Power-save is a good trigger: the device suspends and ALSA repeats underrun//xrun complaints.

## Options, roughly in order of leverage

1. **Redirect fd 2 for the life of the alternate screen (recommended).** `dup` the real stderr to a saved fd, `dup2` a log file (`<user_dir>/stderr.log`, alongside the existing `trace.log` convention in `trace.rs`) onto fd 2 while the TUI is up, restore on teardown. Catches EVERY source — ALSA, PulseAudio, JACK, any C dep — not just the one we know about. Needs `libc` (or `rustix`) as a direct dep of `app`; allowed by the crate rules (only zvm/gvm/scott are dependency-free).
   - Care: Rust panic output goes to fd 2 too, so a panic would land in the log instead of the terminal. Restore fd 2 during teardown and, if the log is non-empty, print its tail after leaving the alternate screen — that also turns today's invisible ALSA spam into something diagnosable.
   - Care: only while the alternate screen is active, so `--help`, CLI errors and the story-picker keep printing normally.
2. **ALSA-specific silencing.** `snd_lib_error_set_handler` with a no-op handler mutes libasound alone. Narrower and unhelpful for other backends, and it means linking libasound directly (the `alsa` crate) rather than through cpal.
3. **Sound off entirely.** Already available as a workaround, not a fix: `enable_sound = false`, or `--no-sound`.

## Workarounds available today

`babelmap … 2>/dev/null` (or `2>/tmp/bm.err` to keep them), or `--no-sound` / `enable_sound = false` if audio isn't wanted in that session.

## Test surface

Hard to reproduce from the outside, since it needs a misbehaving audio device. What IS testable: that the redirect installs and restores (write to fd 2 while active, assert it lands in the log and not the terminal; assert fd 2 is restored afterwards), and that the log's tail is surfaced on exit when non-empty. Platform-gate the fd work to unix; Windows has no ALSA, and the equivalent there would be `SetStdHandle`.
