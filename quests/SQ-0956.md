## The symptom

DOS Zork Zero with the CGA rendition (`zork0.cg1`): the white page bleeds into the artwork. User's clarification, which is the constraint the fix has to respect: **the PC background SHOULD be white for Zork Zero normally — just not for CGA.** So this is not "the IBM PC page is wrong", it is "a two-colour rendition needs a different ground from the one the story asked for".

## Why white is right normally, measured

`machine-screenshots/dos-zorkzero.png` — Zork Zero at the Banquet Hall, 25.7% #FFFFFF and near-whites: WHITE, not blue. The game issues `set_colour(fg=2, bg=9)` on a window the size of the screen, and against a full-colour EGA/MCGA plate that is exactly right. `machine-screenshots/info.txt` records the trace: Arthur and Journey name no colour, Shogun names one on a 548x32 strip, and only Zork Zero colours a window big enough to replace the interpreter's own page.

## Why the CGA rendition is different

`blorb::infocom_pics`'s own documentation, measured across `zork0.cg1`, `arthur.cg1`, `journey.cg1` and `shogun.cg1` — 710 pictures:

  - the PACKED pictures use `{0, 1}` and only `{0, 1}`
  - the BYTE-PER-PIXEL ones use `{0, 2, 3}` and only `{0, 2, 3}`

and `CGA_PALETTE` says what those mean: slot 0 is **black** for the packed form and **transparent** for the byte-per-pixel form; 2 is the archive's white, 3 its black. So a two-colour plate is a STENCIL whose transparency reveals a ground it never stored — and Infocom's own encoding treats the clear bit as BLACK where it stores one at all. `graphics.rs::declines_game_colours` already says this in prose: "honouring it paints the white pillars out against the white page it asked for."

## THE MECHANISM ALREADY EXISTS AND HAS BEEN SWITCHED OFF

SQ-0806's rule is `declines_game_colours() = is_monochrome() && machine_pair.is_none()`. `machine_pair` is `Config::machine_default_colours`, which is gated on `ProfileSource::licenses_machine_colours`: **Medium => true**, Asked => opt-in, Fallback => false.

SQ-0928 gave `InterpreterProfile::IbmPc` a pair (blue 6 under white 9). So:

  - bare `zork0-r393-s890714.z6` + `--pictures zork0.cg1` -> Fallback -> no pair -> rule FIRES -> ground goes to the host theme -> looks fine on a dark terminal
  - the DOS press (a real medium) -> **Medium -> licensed -> pair is Some -> rule does NOT fire** -> the white page is honoured -> bleed

That is the report, and it is the launch CGA art actually comes from. The `Asked` route (launch-options dialog with system colours opted in) reaches it too.

The existing doc anticipated half of this — "asking the profile directly would stop this rule firing for every CGA stencil opened off a plain .z6" — so it guarded against consulting the PROFILE, but `machine_default_colours` still answers `Some` on a real medium.

## A fix that must NOT be taken

Do not simply drop the `machine_pair.is_none()` guard, and do not make the machine page win for mono archives. Both break the Macintosh, deliberately decided in SQ-0838/0846: the Mac's mono `Pic.data` is the archive that machine's own interpreter chose FOR its white page, in one decision (`mac/xzip.lst`'s `SetColor := (zWHITE*256) + zBLACK`), and turning colours off there cost SQ-0846's status banner its ink. Making the machine page win would also give CGA the IBM PC's BLUE, which is not what the evidence above points at either.

The shape that fits all three facts is narrower: a two-colour CGA archive's ground is the archive's OWN black — not the story's page, not the theme's backdrop, not the machine's blue — while a Mac mono plate keeps its machine's white. The discriminator is which machine the archive belongs to, which `hardware_palette()` already answers.

## Verify before implementing

The claim that a CGA graphics screen's ground is black is supported in-repo by `CGA_PALETTE` slot 0, but the HARDWARE claim (CGA 640x200 two-colour mode having a fixed black background with a selectable foreground) is from memory and CLAUDE.md forbids that — check it against an authoritative source first.

Also note the corpus gap: there is **no CGA-rendition v6 capture** in `machine-screenshots/`. `dos-hitchhiker.png` is a CGA colour display but a v3 story, and its page is #0F009E — the interpreter's blue, 92.4% of the frame — which is a TEXT-mode page and says nothing directly about a v6 graphics screen. Worth asking the user for a capture of Zork Zero running the CGA plates before pinning a colour.

## "This makes another special case for DOS/PC" — the user's objection, and why the shape should be the reverse

The objection is right about the smell and, I think, wrong about the direction — because the special case is ALREADY in the code and this is the chance to remove it rather than add one.

`is_monochrome()` is a BOOLEAN, and the question it is standing in for is "which machine's two-colour mode is this?". Its own doc says so outright: "the Macintosh's monochrome `Pic.data` answers yes under exactly the same test as a `.cg1`". So two archives from two different machines, whose grounds are genuinely different — the Mac's white (from `mac/xzip.lst`'s `SetColor := (zWHITE*256) + zBLACK`) and CGA's black (from `CGA_PALETTE` slot 0) — collapse to one bit, and every caller downstream has to guess which one it is holding. `declines_game_colours` guesses with `machine_pair.is_none()`, and that guess is exactly what SQ-0928 broke.

Stated generally, with no machine named: **a rendition belongs to a machine, and the ground a two-colour stencil reveals belongs to that machine's display mode — not to the story, which cannot see which archive was loaded.** Zork Zero issues the same `set_colour(fg=2, bg=9)` for every video card alike; that is the whole reason the story is the wrong authority here.

Under that rule the four cases fall out without any of them being special:

  - Mac mono `Pic.data`  -> Macintosh   -> white ground   (already correct today)
  - DOS `.CG1`           -> IBM PC CGA  -> black ground   (the reported defect)
  - DOS `.EG1` / `.MG1`  -> IBM PC EGA/MCGA -> full-colour plate, no transparency to fill, the story's white stands (correct today, and what the user means by "normally white")
  - Amiga `Pic.data`     -> Amiga       -> its own defaults (already correct today)

The discriminator needed for this ALREADY EXISTS and is already general: `hardware_palette()` returns `Some(CGA_PALETTE)`, `Some(EGA_PALETTE)`, the Apple II table, or `None`. It is the function `is_monochrome()` throws information away from. So the change is to stop flattening it — ask which two-colour machine, not whether it is two-colour — and the `machine_pair.is_none()` guess can then go, which is the actual special case.

Net: one guess removed, no new per-machine branch, and the Mac keeps working for the reason it works today rather than by accident.

## THE CORPUS GAP IS CLOSED — `machine-screenshots/dos-zorkzero-cga.png`

The user ran Zork Zero in a DOS emulator in CGA mode and captured it. This is the v6 CGA frame the note above said did not exist, and it settles the colour question with a machine instead of an inference.

Censused whole, 507x317, at the Banquet Hall with `[MORE]` showing:

    48.3%  #000000                    <- the page
     8.8%  #A0A0A0                    <- the ink
    161 distinct colours              <- a grey ramp from video scaling, no second hue

Row parity checked first, because `st-zork1.png` taught this corpus that an interlaced capture censuses backwards (SQ-0933): even rows 39,252 black / 7,135 grey, odd rows 38,391 / 6,968. They agree, so this frame is NOT interlaced and the whole-frame census is the honest one.

## What it says, in colour NUMBERS rather than shades

The page is **0 (black)** and the ink is **7 (light grey)** — `#A0A0A0` is the same value `dos-hitchhiker.png` shows for its ink, and `zvm::interpreter`'s IBM PC row already records `#AAAAAA` as EGA/CGA entry 7. Two captures from the same emulator family agreeing on colour 7 is what makes this a colour number and not a shade, which is the form this repo pins (see info.txt's RGB note).

## AND IT IS THE STORY'S PAIR INVERTED, WHICH IS THE REAL FINDING

Zork Zero issues `set_colour(fg=2, bg=9)` — **black ink on a white page** — for every video card alike. `machine-screenshots/dos-zorkzero.png` shows exactly that honoured on the colour rendition: 25.7% #FFFFFF, and the user confirms white is correct there.

The CGA capture is the opposite polarity: **light ink on a black page.** So a two-colour display does not merely substitute a different background — it does not honour the story's pair AT ALL, which is precisely what `graphics.rs::declines_game_colours` already asserts in prose ("a two-colour display has no colours to give a story at all") and what SQ-0806 exists for. The defect is that SQ-0928's machine pair switched that rule off on the medium where CGA art actually lives.

## This also confirms the polarity is per MACHINE, not per "is it two-colour"

    Macintosh mono `Pic.data`  ->  WHITE page, BLACK ink   (mac/xzip.lst: SetColor := (zWHITE*256) + zBLACK)
    IBM PC `.CG1`              ->  BLACK page, LIGHT ink   (this capture)

Two two-colour archives, opposite polarity, and `is_monochrome()` answers `true` for both — its own doc says the Mac's mono plate passes the identical test as a `.cg1`. That is the flattening described in the previous note, now with a measurement on each side of it rather than an argument. `hardware_palette()` already distinguishes them.

One loose end for whoever implements: `CGA_PALETTE` slot 1 (the packed SET bit) is `[255, 255, 255]` pure white, while this capture's ink is colour 7 light grey. Both are defensible — mode 6's foreground is selectable — but the table and the capture should be reconciled deliberately rather than by accident, and the note in `info.txt` about pinning numbers rather than shades applies.
