## The symptom

DOS Zork Zero with the CGA rendition (`zork0.cg1`): the white page bleeds into the artwork. User's clarification, which is the constraint the fix has to respect: **the PC background SHOULD be white for Zork Zero normally — just not for CGA.** So this is not "the IBM PC page is wrong", it is "a two-colour rendition needs a different ground from the one the story asked for".

## Why white is right normally, measured

`machine-screenshots/dos-zorkzero.png` — Zork Zero at the Banquet Hall, 25.7% #FFFFFF and near-whites: WHITE, not blue. The game issues `set_colour(fg=2, bg=9)` on a window the size of the screen, and against a full-colour EGA/MCGA plate that is exactly right. `machine-screenshots/info.txt` records the trace: Arthur and Journey name no colour, Shogun names one on a 548x32 strip, and only Zork Zero colours a window big enough to replace the interpreter's own page.

## Why the CGA rendition is different

`blorb::infocom_pics`'s own documentation, measured across `zork0.cg1`, `arthur.cg1`, `journey.cg1` and `shogun.cg1` — 710 pictures:

  - the PACKED pictures use `{0, 1}` and only `{0, 1}`
  - the BYTE-PER-PIXEL ones use `{0, 2, 3}` and only `{0, 2, 3}`

and `CGA_PALETTE` says what those mean: slot 0 is **black** for the packed form and **transparent** for the byte-per-pixel form; 2 is the archive's white, 3 its black. So a two-colour plate is a STENCIL whose transparency reveals a ground it never stored — and Infocom's own encoding treats the clear bit as BLACK where it stores one at all. `graphics.rs::declines_game_colours` already says this in prose: "honouring it paints the white pillars out against the white page it asked for."

## THE MECHANISM ALREADY EXISTS AND HAS BEEN SWITCHED OFF

SQ-0806's rule is `declines_game_colours() = is_monochrome() && machine_pair.is_none()`. `machine_pair` is `Config::machine_default_colours`, which is gated on `ProfileSource::licenses_machine_colours`: **Medium => true**, Asked => opt-in, Fallback => false.

SQ-0928 gave `InterpreterProfile::IbmPc` a pair (blue 6 under white 9). So:

  - bare `zork0-r393-s890714.z6` + `--pictures zork0.cg1` -> Fallback -> no pair -> rule FIRES -> ground goes to the host theme -> looks fine on a dark terminal
  - the DOS press (a real medium) -> **Medium -> licensed -> pair is Some -> rule does NOT fire** -> the white page is honoured -> bleed

That is the report, and it is the launch CGA art actually comes from. The `Asked` route (launch-options dialog with system colours opted in) reaches it too.

The existing doc anticipated half of this — "asking the profile directly would stop this rule firing for every CGA stencil opened off a plain .z6" — so it guarded against consulting the PROFILE, but `machine_default_colours` still answers `Some` on a real medium.

## A fix that must NOT be taken

Do not simply drop the `machine_pair.is_none()` guard, and do not make the machine page win for mono archives. Both break the Macintosh, deliberately decided in SQ-0838/0846: the Mac's mono `Pic.data` is the archive that machine's own interpreter chose FOR its white page, in one decision (`mac/xzip.lst`'s `SetColor := (zWHITE*256) + zBLACK`), and turning colours off there cost SQ-0846's status banner its ink. Making the machine page win would also give CGA the IBM PC's BLUE, which is not what the evidence above points at either.

The shape that fits all three facts is narrower: a two-colour CGA archive's ground is the archive's OWN black — not the story's page, not the theme's backdrop, not the machine's blue — while a Mac mono plate keeps its machine's white. The discriminator is which machine the archive belongs to, which `hardware_palette()` already answers.

## Verify before implementing

The claim that a CGA graphics screen's ground is black is supported in-repo by `CGA_PALETTE` slot 0, but the HARDWARE claim (CGA 640x200 two-colour mode having a fixed black background with a selectable foreground) is from memory and CLAUDE.md forbids that — check it against an authoritative source first.

Also note the corpus gap: there is **no CGA-rendition v6 capture** in `machine-screenshots/`. `dos-hitchhiker.png` is a CGA colour display but a v3 story, and its page is #0F009E — the interpreter's blue, 92.4% of the frame — which is a TEXT-mode page and says nothing directly about a v6 graphics screen. Worth asking the user for a capture of Zork Zero running the CGA plates before pinning a colour.

## "This makes another special case for DOS/PC" — the user's objection, and why the shape should be the reverse

The objection is right about the smell and, I think, wrong about the direction — because the special case is ALREADY in the code and this is the chance to remove it rather than add one.

`is_monochrome()` is a BOOLEAN, and the question it is standing in for is "which machine's two-colour mode is this?". Its own doc says so outright: "the Macintosh's monochrome `Pic.data` answers yes under exactly the same test as a `.cg1`". So two archives from two different machines, whose grounds are genuinely different — the Mac's white (from `mac/xzip.lst`'s `SetColor := (zWHITE*256) + zBLACK`) and CGA's black (from `CGA_PALETTE` slot 0) — collapse to one bit, and every caller downstream has to guess which one it is holding. `declines_game_colours` guesses with `machine_pair.is_none()`, and that guess is exactly what SQ-0928 broke.

Stated generally, with no machine named: **a rendition belongs to a machine, and the ground a two-colour stencil reveals belongs to that machine's display mode — not to the story, which cannot see which archive was loaded.** Zork Zero issues the same `set_colour(fg=2, bg=9)` for every video card alike; that is the whole reason the story is the wrong authority here.

Under that rule the four cases fall out without any of them being special:

  - Mac mono `Pic.data`  -> Macintosh   -> white ground   (already correct today)
  - DOS `.CG1`           -> IBM PC CGA  -> black ground   (the reported defect)
  - DOS `.EG1` / `.MG1`  -> IBM PC EGA/MCGA -> full-colour plate, no transparency to fill, the story's white stands (correct today, and what the user means by "normally white")
  - Amiga `Pic.data`     -> Amiga       -> its own defaults (already correct today)

The discriminator needed for this ALREADY EXISTS and is already general: `hardware_palette()` returns `Some(CGA_PALETTE)`, `Some(EGA_PALETTE)`, the Apple II table, or `None`. It is the function `is_monochrome()` throws information away from. So the change is to stop flattening it — ask which two-colour machine, not whether it is two-colour — and the `machine_pair.is_none()` guess can then go, which is the actual special case.

Net: one guess removed, no new per-machine branch, and the Mac keeps working for the reason it works today rather than by accident.

## THE CORPUS GAP IS CLOSED — `machine-screenshots/dos-zorkzero-cga.png`

The user ran Zork Zero in a DOS emulator in CGA mode and captured it. This is the v6 CGA frame the note above said did not exist, and it settles the colour question with a machine instead of an inference.

Censused whole, 507x317, at the Banquet Hall with `[MORE]` showing:

    48.3%  #000000                    <- the page
     8.8%  #A0A0A0                    <- the ink
    161 distinct colours              <- a grey ramp from video scaling, no second hue

Row parity checked first, because `st-zork1.png` taught this corpus that an interlaced capture censuses backwards (SQ-0933): even rows 39,252 black / 7,135 grey, odd rows 38,391 / 6,968. They agree, so this frame is NOT interlaced and the whole-frame census is the honest one.

## What it says, in colour NUMBERS rather than shades

The page is **0 (black)** and the ink is **7 (light grey)** — `#A0A0A0` is the same value `dos-hitchhiker.png` shows for its ink, and `zvm::interpreter`'s IBM PC row already records `#AAAAAA` as EGA/CGA entry 7. Two captures from the same emulator family agreeing on colour 7 is what makes this a colour number and not a shade, which is the form this repo pins (see info.txt's RGB note).

## AND IT IS THE STORY'S PAIR INVERTED, WHICH IS THE REAL FINDING

Zork Zero issues `set_colour(fg=2, bg=9)` — **black ink on a white page** — for every video card alike. `machine-screenshots/dos-zorkzero.png` shows exactly that honoured on the colour rendition: 25.7% #FFFFFF, and the user confirms white is correct there.

The CGA capture is the opposite polarity: **light ink on a black page.** So a two-colour display does not merely substitute a different background — it does not honour the story's pair AT ALL, which is precisely what `graphics.rs::declines_game_colours` already asserts in prose ("a two-colour display has no colours to give a story at all") and what SQ-0806 exists for. The defect is that SQ-0928's machine pair switched that rule off on the medium where CGA art actually lives.

## This also confirms the polarity is per MACHINE, not per "is it two-colour"

    Macintosh mono `Pic.data`  ->  WHITE page, BLACK ink   (mac/xzip.lst: SetColor := (zWHITE*256) + zBLACK)
    IBM PC `.CG1`              ->  BLACK page, LIGHT ink   (this capture)

Two two-colour archives, opposite polarity, and `is_monochrome()` answers `true` for both — its own doc says the Mac's mono plate passes the identical test as a `.cg1`. That is the flattening described in the previous note, now with a measurement on each side of it rather than an argument. `hardware_palette()` already distinguishes them.

One loose end for whoever implements: `CGA_PALETTE` slot 1 (the packed SET bit) is `[255, 255, 255]` pure white, while this capture's ink is colour 7 light grey. Both are defensible — mode 6's foreground is selectable — but the table and the capture should be reconciled deliberately rather than by accident, and the note in `info.txt` about pinning numbers rather than shades applies.

## THE DESIGN, with the numbers checked rather than argued

`InterpreterProfile::default_colours()` returns `(background, foreground)` in ZMSD §8.3.1 colour numbers. Read out of `zvm::interpreter`:

    IBM PC     bg 6 (blue)   fg 9 (white)     IBM_PC_DEFAULT_BACKGROUND / _FOREGROUND
    Macintosh  bg 9 (white)  fg 2 (black)     MAC_DEFAULT_BACKGROUND / _FOREGROUND

Now put the two two-colour archives beside them:

    Macintosh mono Pic.data  ->  bg 9, fg 2   ==  IDENTICAL to the Mac profile's pair
    IBM PC .CG1              ->  bg 2, fg 9   !=  the IbmPc profile's (6, 9)

**That is the whole change, and it is one channel.** A two-colour CGA rendition moves the BACKGROUND from blue 6 to black 2 and leaves the ink at white 9. The capture agrees: page #000000, ink #A0A0A0, which is the same shade `dos-hitchhiker.png` measures for its ink and which the IBM PC row already records as white 9 rendered by that emulator family.

**And the Macintosh needs no special case at all** — its mono archive states exactly what its profile already states, so a rule of the form "the archive's pair when it has one, else the profile's" leaves the Mac byte-for-byte unchanged BY CONSTRUCTION rather than by an exemption. That is the "removes a special case rather than adding one" claim, now verified.

## Two parts, and the second is what makes the first safe

1. **A two-colour archive declines the STORY's colours** — `honor_game_colours = false` — with the `machine_pair.is_none()` guard DROPPED. A two-colour display has two pixel states and maps ink to the set bit and page to the clear bit, so a story's named pair is unrepresentable, not merely overridden. Zork Zero asks for bg 9 / fg 2 and the CGA capture shows bg 2 / fg 9 — literally inverted, which is what "unrepresentable" looks like on screen.

2. **The archive supplies the machine pair for its display mode**, which becomes the ground. This is what SQ-0846 was missing. Its objection to dropping the guard was correct AT THE TIME: declining left the Mac with NO ground (it fell through to the host theme) and cost SQ-0846's status banner its ink. With the archive stating (9, 2) for the Mac and (2, 9) for CGA, declining no longer throws a ground away — it replaces the story's with the display's. Both cases land right for the same reason.

## What must NOT change

- The LICENCE gate stays exactly as it is. `machine_colours_licensed()` is `Medium => true, Asked => opt-in, Fallback => false`. A bare `.z6` opened with `--pictures zork0.cg1` is Fallback, so it gets no machine pair, honour goes off, and the ground falls to the host theme — today's SQ-0806 behaviour, and the existing pins for it must keep passing.
- The one-run PIN on `HONOR_GAME_COLOURS` (SQ-0646/0807) and the `AppState::artwork_declines_colours` mirror (SQ-0860) both stay; they solve different problems and neither is what this quest is about.
- `CGA_PALETTE` slot 1 stays pure white unless there is a reason to move it; the §8.3.1 pair is the thing being stated here, and the shade is the emulator's (see `info.txt`'s RGB notes).

## Where the discriminator already lives

`blorb::infocom_pics::InfocomPics::hardware_palette()` already answers `Some(CGA_PALETTE)` / `Some(EGA_PALETTE)` / the Apple II table / `None`. `is_monochrome()` is `hardware_palette() == Some(CGA_PALETTE)` — the boolean that throws the machine away. The Mac's mono plate and a `.cg1` both answer `true` to it, by its own documentation, which is the flattening this fixes.

Note the naming trap for whoever implements: `CGA_PALETTE` is the two-colour table shared by BOTH the Mac mono plate and the `.cg1`, so its name does not tell you which machine you have. The medium/profile does.

## THE SYMPTOM, stated by the user in the form that should be tested

"The black background punches through the art in the transparent areas, where our art is missing a lot of details as those remain painted white."

So the visible defect is not "the ground is the wrong colour" — it is **lost detail**. The `.cg1` plates are light line work with large transparent regions. On the machine those regions are black and the line work reads against them. In lanthorn they are painted with the white page Zork Zero asked for, so light-on-light, and a great deal of the picture simply is not there.

This matters for how the fix is pinned. A colour-equality assertion ("the resolved ground is black") passes the moment one pixel is right and says nothing about the thing the user reported. A DETAIL assertion — pixels distinguishable from the ground, or distinct colours, inside the art region — fails for the actual reason, and both numbers should be recorded so a later reader can see the margin.

It also confirms the diagnosis end to end rather than by inference: `Picture::rgba_with` drops the transparent index to alpha 0, and the hole is resolved against the page of the moment by `v6_layout::flatten_onto_page` (raster), `fill_story_page_clear` / `fill_window_pages` (hybrid canvas) and `inline_image::flatten_onto` (story floats). Correcting the page corrects all three — provided each of them takes the corrected page rather than holding its own copy of it, which is worth checking rather than assuming.

Falsification should therefore reproduce the DETAIL LOSS, not merely a different ground colour.

## REOPENED — right symptom, wrong mechanism. The user's framing supersedes the design above.

> "we should honor what the game tells us, but at initial boot we need to configure our colors to default to dark as background (so that gets reported to zork-zero)"
> "so, cga has a different machine colors than ega"

That is the correct reading and it makes everything above simpler. This was never a story-versus-archive conflict to be resolved by declining. **The video card is part of the machine**, and an IBM PC with a CGA card has a different §8.3.1 default pair from one with EGA:

    IBM PC + EGA/MCGA   bg 6 (blue)   fg 9 (white)
    IBM PC + CGA        bg 2 (black)  fg 9 (white)

The pair is REPORTED to the story at boot — `Config::machine_default_colours()` -> `startup.rs`'s `host_default_colours` -> `Machine::set_default_colours(bg, fg)` -> header `$2C`/`$2D`. Zork Zero reads it, works from it, and its `color` command swaps from there.

## What must change from what shipped in 92f2fba7

1. **STOP declining game colours for a two-colour rendition.** That is what killed the `color` command, which the user reports as a regression. `honor_game_colours` stays ON.
2. **Report the CGA pair instead**, so `(2, 9)` reaches `$2C`/`$2D` rather than `(6, 9)`.

`InterpreterProfile::two_colour_colours()` already computes `(2, 9)` for `IbmPc` and `Config::machine_two_colour_colours()` already licenses it under the same gate — 92f2fba7 built both. Only their CONSUMER changes: today they feed a comparison inside `declines_game_colours`; they should feed the reported pair.

## Why this is strictly better than what shipped

- **`color` works again.** The regression goes away rather than being accepted.
- **The ground is the machine's, not the theme's.** 92f2fba7's ground fell through to the host theme, which resolves near-black on a dark terminal and matches the capture BY COINCIDENCE — on a light terminal the stencil would wash out again. Reporting the pair fixes it for every terminal.
- **The hard part evaporates.** The lane's stated reason for deferring "publish the machine ground" was that `machine_screen_pair` gates on Flags 1 bit 0, which `honor_game_colours = false` clears, and five other sites gate on the live honour flag. With honour left ON, none of that arises.
- **The Macintosh still needs no exemption.** Its mono plate states `(9, 2)`, identical to its profile, so a rule of "the card's pair when the archive names one, else the machine's" leaves it unchanged by construction — the same property the one-channel design had.

## What to keep from 92f2fba7

The measurement apparatus, all of it: `v6_cga_stencil_page`'s DETAIL oracle (1,115 lit cells / 251 colours on white against 1,672 / 256 on black; raster 2 colours against 3), the 360K disk-1 reproducer that needs no `--pictures`, and `the_raster_composite_and_the_floats_take_the_same_page_as_the_ring`. Those assertions should survive the mechanism change — if any of them now needs loosening, that is a signal the new mechanism is not reaching a surface the old one did.

Also keep the softened wording about two states and the swap; it is still accurate.

## And this may close SQ-0957 for free

SQ-0957 is "transparent pixels do not follow a colour change". Its cause was diagnosed as the decline PINNING the ground so `@set_colour` never moved it. With honour left on and the pair merely seeded dark, a `color` swap moves the ground for real — and the transparent pixels resolve against whatever the page is now. Re-test it once this lands before spending anything on it.

## "CGA should be light grey, not white" — measured, and it is TWO changes

Sampled `machine-screenshots/dos-zorkzero-cga.png` by region:

    status text (top)    (160,160,160)  1850 px
    left pillar art      (160,160,160)  1570 px

**The same shade for the TEXT INK and the ARTWORK.** The card has exactly two colours — black and light grey — and everything on the screen is one or the other. #A0A0A0 is the emulator's rendering of EGA entry 7, #AAAAAA, the same value `dos-hitchhiker.png` measures for its ink and the value `zvm::interpreter`'s IBM PC row already records.

Today lanthorn produces pure white on both paths, for two independent reasons, and BOTH must move:

### 1. The text path — `zvm::screen::ega_true_colour`

    9 if yzip => 0x7FFF,   // white  EGA 15  #FFFFFF
    9         => 0x56B5,   // white  EGA 7   #AAAAAA

Colour 9 is the ONE entry the two IBM interpreters disagree on, and v6 selects `IbmYzip` (SQ-0939's `palette_for(number, zversion)`), so a v6 story gets pure white. A CGA rendition must resolve white 9 to `0x56B5` — the xzip value — because that is what the card had. Note this is a per-CARD fact sitting next to a per-INTERPRETER one, which is exactly the distinction this quest is about.

### 2. The art path — `blorb::infocom_pics::CGA_PALETTE`

    [255, 255, 255],  // 1: packed set bit
    [255, 255, 255],  // 2: the archive's white

Both should be `#AAAAAA`. This is the loose end an earlier note flagged as "deliberately unreconciled" — the capture now reconciles it. Slot 0 (black / transparent) and slot 3 (the archive's black) stay `#000000`.

`CGA_PALETTE` is shared with the Macintosh's mono `Pic.data`, so **check the Mac before changing it in place.** The Mac's plate is black ink on a white page (`mac/xzip.lst`'s `SetColor := (zWHITE*256) + zBLACK`) and its white is a real white, not a card's grey. If the two disagree — and they probably do — the table has to become per-card rather than one shared constant, which is the same "the card is part of the machine" move as the pair itself. `machine-screenshots/mac-zorkzero-bw.jpg` is the capture to measure against before deciding.

## THE FULL CORRECTED DESIGN, superseding what shipped in 92f2fba7

    IBM PC + EGA/MCGA   bg 6 blue    fg 9 white -> #FFFFFF (yzip, unchanged)
    IBM PC + CGA        bg 2 black   fg 9 white -> #AAAAAA (light grey)
    Macintosh mono      bg 9 white   fg 2 black  (unchanged, states it once)

  1. Do NOT decline game colours for a two-colour rendition — that killed the `color` command.
  2. Report the CGA pair at boot so `(2, 9)` reaches `$2C`/`$2D` and Zork Zero works from a dark default.
  3. Resolve white 9 to `#AAAAAA` on a CGA rendition, text and art alike.
