--- note 2026-08-19T03:06:50Z ---

Root cause: a Macintosh sample has TWO silences. 0x80 is silence while the sound channel is driven; 0x00 is the machine's actual rest position, because the Mac's sound output is a unipolar PWM where "no pulse" is the speaker at rest. So a sample that simply began at its DC would slam the speaker off rest and click — and thirteen of Sherlock's fifteen effects therefore open at 0x00, ramp linearly to full drive, play, and ramp back down.

SQ-0921 subtracted a flat 0x80, which turns both ramps into an excursion to FULL NEGATIVE: a pop at the start and another at the end. Correct decode is `sample - 128*env(i)`, which equals `env(i) * (sample - 128)` — the same fade, about silence instead of about full drive.

The ramp is 1/150 s at each end, measured not assumed:
- Seven of Sherlock's effects (3,4,5,6,9,15,16) are the same recording at the same rate on both discs, so the envelope can be fitted against the Amiga master. Fitting ramp length N puts `rate/N` inside [149.97, 151.11] on every one — 150 is the only round figure fitting all seven.
- With N = rate/150, effect 9 reconstructs from the Amiga bytes with ZERO error; the rest to within 1-2 counts in 256.
- The clincher that the trapezoid is the whole story and not merely a shape that fits: under a flat 0x80 the number of bytes by which a Mac effect disagreed with its Amiga twin is EXACTLY twice the fitted ramp length (e3: 204 vs N=102; e9: 128 vs N=64; e4: 136 vs 68; e5: 204 vs 102; e15: 128 vs 64), and **zero outside the ramps**. The Mac master is the Amiga master times a trapezoid and nothing else. (e17 is a genuinely different take and is excluded; e8 and e14 carry no ramp at all.)

Effects 8 and 14 open/close mid-signal (0x93, 0x9F) — the mastering pass missed them, so they click on a real Macintosh too. Detected by the only signature that cannot fire by accident: a ramped file begins AND ends at exactly full negative, which no recording does. Getting this backwards would push those two OFF silence rather than onto it.

Fix: `to_signed(&mut samples, rate)` in blorb::infocom_sound, integer throughout — `bias = (128 * min(i, n-1-i) * 150 / rate).min(128)`, or a flat 128 when unramped.

Tests: two unit cases (a synthetic ramped payload at 1500 Hz, where the ramp is exactly 10 samples and the arithmetic is hand-checkable; and an unramped one staying flat), plus two real-media assertions — every effect starts and ends within 32 counts of silence, and outside a `rate/150` ramp each same-length effect is byte-identical to the Amiga's.

Falsified: with the ramp detection stubbed to false, the real-media case fails with "effect 3 steps to -128/-128 against silence at its ends" — the reported symptom exactly. The falsification also caught a latent bug in the new assertion: `i8::abs()` overflows on precisely the -128 it was guarding against, so it widens to i32 first.

Note the ramp boundary in a test must round UP (`div_ceil(150)`): the decoder reaches full drive on the first sample that divides out to 128, which is 103 at 15360 Hz, not 102. Two frames failed on the first attempt for exactly this reason.
