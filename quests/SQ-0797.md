--- note 2026-08-12T07:07:18Z ---

MEASURED 2026-08-12 on `stories/zork0-r393-s890714.z6` with `pictures = "zork0.eg1"`, after SQ-0794 put the IBM EGA table under it.

EGA has no bronze, so Zork Zero's artist made one: the proscenium arch is a column-by-column DITHER of bright red (index 12, `255,85,85`) against brown (index 6, `170,85,0`) — 22496 and 18202 pixels of the boot frame respectively. On the card that stored it those columns are half as wide as an MCGA pixel and the eye fuses them into bronze. Bocfel says the same thing in its own words, about Zork Zero's EGA hint background: "renders … as a two-colour dither, so no single pixel of the artwork is the colour the eye actually sees: sampling gives the bright magenta while the field reads as a muted mauve" (`z6/draw_border.cpp:745`).

babelmap keeps all 640 columns. SQ-0790's `PictSource::art_scale()` maps a 640-wide plate at (1, 2), which is geometrically right — it covers the same rectangle as a 320-wide one — but every art pixel survives as a distinct unit pixel, so the dither reaches the screen at full contrast and the arch reads salmon-and-olive speckle instead of bronze. A 320-wide MCGA or Amiga plate doubles to (2, 2) and has no dither at that frequency, so this is specific to the 640-wide renditions.

Confirmed by simulation rather than argument: box-filter the rendered EGA frame's column pairs (what half-width pixels do) and the arch is unmistakably bronze — scratchpad `sq0794/07-ega-AFTER-as-displayed.png` beside the raw `02-ega-AFTER.png`. Mean per-channel distance to the MCGA frame at that resolution drops from 10.51 to 9.02 with SQ-0794's brown, and the remaining gap is the two renditions being separately drawn art.

NOT a palette bug and NOT SQ-0794's business — the pixels and the colours are both now exactly what the archive says. The open question is where the blend belongs: a horizontal pre-filter when `art_scale().0 == 1`, or leaving it to the renderer's own scale to the terminal pane (which blurs when the pane is narrower than 640px and does nothing when it is wider — i.e. it is currently a function of the user's terminal width, which is the part that is wrong). Whichever, it must not touch CGA, whose 640-wide art is genuine one-bit line work that blending would only make grey.
