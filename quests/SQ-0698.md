INVESTIGATED 2026-08-09 — premise does not hold; reframe before acting. (1) SPEC: ZMSD says NOTHING about scaling/stretching/tiling. Sect 8.8.6.1 is the only picture-drawing text ('Pictures can be drawn or erased... picture_data... specify its height and width'); no semantics for a picture that does not match its target. So neither tiling NOR scaling is standard-sanctioned — both are pure interpreter policy. (2) WHAT ZORK0 ACTUALLY DRAWS (measured, gameplay state in the Great Hall): win7 canvas 640x400 takes exactly THREE draws — pic 5 (banner, raw 320x34, 100% opaque) at (0,0); pic 497 (LEFT pillar, raw 36x166, 4200/5976 = ~70% opaque) at x=0,y=68; pic 498 (RIGHT pillar, raw 37x166, ~68% opaque) at x=566,y=68. Each drawn ONCE. (3) MY FIRST READING WAS WRONG and is corrected here: I compared RAW art dims against UNIT-space screen coords. V6_ART_SCALE = 2 (session.rs:170), so 36x166 is drawn as 72x332 — placed at y=68 it reaches y=400 exactly. Row-by-row alpha scan of the composited canvas confirms opaque flank pixels at EVERY sampled row from 80 to 380, columns 14..627. The flanks already span the full frame; there is no gap, so there is NOTHING to tile. (4) ART SHAPE: extracted 497/498 to PNG and looked — a copper fluted column WITH A CAPITAL AND BASE. The ends are not interchangeable, so vertical repetition would seam visibly. It is authored as a complete object, not a tile. (5) METHOD WARNING: do not read the op list from GameSession::display_list() — that is the ARCHIVE DTO and drops windows whose replay did not reproduce their canvas. Use the live display_ops / a pixel measurement. (6) CONCLUSION: our stretch_flanks (BottomPlan::Frame/Menu, SQ-0511) is not stretching a short motif over a long gap — it handles pane-aspect letterbox slack, a display-fitting decision. If Spatterlight looks richer it is almost certainly PER-GAME ENHANCEMENT (it carries known game-specific work for the Infocom v6 titles), not a different reading of the format. Remaining hypothesis worth testing instead of tiling: whether we apply the x2 scale consistently, or whether a non-integer letterbox factor resamples the pillars and reads as blurry. DECISION NEEDED: does babelmap want per-game enhancement at all? That is a policy change from the faithful-reproduction line held elsewhere (e.g. the Arthur erase kept as-is).

THE DISCARD STANDS, now on the authoritative source (the game's own opcodes) rather than the lossy archive DTO. Zork Zero's boot trace, verbatim: '@picture_data(497) -> h=332, w=72' then '@draw_picture(number=497, window=7, y=69, x=1)'; '@picture_data(498) -> h=332, w=74' then '@draw_picture(number=498, window=7, y=69, x=567)'. Three things this settles that my first pass could only infer: (1) THE GAME ITSELF SEES 332, not 166 — picture_data returns the UNIT-space (x2) height, so the game and the interpreter agree on the size, and my earlier raw-vs-unit confusion was never a discrepancy the game could observe. (2) y=69 + h=332 = 401 on a 400-tall screen, so ONE draw covers the whole frame height exactly; there is no leftover band for tiling to fill. (3) The game QUERIED the size before placing (picture_data), and elsewhere in the same trace it queries window geometry (get_wind_prop prop 2/3) — so it demonstrably adapts when it wants to, and here it chose a single full-height draw. No loop, no repeat, no second draw of either pillar anywhere in the trace. Contrast with SQ-0697, where tracing the ops DID overturn my conclusion: there the game issued per-line set_cursor that the emitted text never revealed. Here the ops corroborate rather than contradict.   UPLOADS   both side columns: 48x792px RGBA (ids 456006487 and 463451546)
  CELLS     48/8 = 6 cols, 792/18 = 44 rows
  PLACED    left  rows 18..61 cols 1..6     (6 cols x 44 rows, 264 cells)
            right rows 18..61 cols 110..115 (6 cols x 44 rows, 264 cells)

**The placement rect matches its image EXACTLY.** Nothing is clipped, nothing is short, and the geometry is not the defect — which rules out the whole family of explanations that fit SQ-0742's flank shortfall (bands stopping above the frame, a clip against a stale viewport, a rounding of native pixel height).

What stops early is the CONTENT INSIDE the canvas we composite and upload: the rules run roughly three-quarters down the 792px strip and the remainder is empty. The rendered frame (`/tmp/a3.png`, sent to the user) shows it plainly, and shows a second detail that a scaling bug would not produce: **the right strip carries two vertical rules of DIFFERENT heights**, the inner one running visibly lower than the outer. Two rules in one 48px-wide canvas ending at different rows is what you get when each is drawn from its own source element, not when one canvas is scaled.

HYPOTHESIS, this quest's original one, now with evidence: the game supplies a UNIT of border decoration meant to REPEAT down the column (Spatterlight appears to tile), and we scale or stamp a single copy into the strip, leaving the rest of the canvas unpainted.
