## The trick, and why it should work

Half-blocks resolves an image to `cols x (rows * 2)` SAMPLES — `ratatui-image`'s primitive encoder does `img.resize_exact(size.width, size.height * 2)`. So shrinking the terminal font buys resolution directly: more cells, more samples. And in RASTER mode the game's own text is rasterised INTO the composite rather than drawn as terminal glyphs, so it sharpens along with the art instead of becoming unreadable. Only lanthorn's own chrome stays as tiny glyphs.

At 640 cols x 200 rows that is 640x400 samples — the v6 native screen at 1:1. That is the user's insight and it is correct.

## What stops it

    const MAX_V6_UPSCALE: f64 = 2.0;

`v6_fit_source` pre-scales the composite by Nearest, capped at that multiple, and then hands it to the protocol whose `Resize::Fit` **only ever shrinks** and centres. So past the cap the image is frozen at 1280x800 (the 640x400 canvas doubled) while the pane keeps growing, and the game is centred in an ever-larger field of margin. That is exactly the reported symptom — "instead of increasing in resolution as the font size gets smaller, the game window itself just gets smaller".

In cells, under the half-block picker's own 10x20 geometry, the cap is reached at **128 x 40**. Beyond that every extra cell is margin.

## Why the cap does not apply to this backend

Its own doc states the cost it is defending against:

> A native 320x200 game therefore encodes at most 1280x800 instead of the full pane device resolution (which could be ~1920x1200 = 9 MB and cost hundreds of ms to resize+PNG-encode).

That is a **kitty** cost — a PNG built and uploaded over the wire. **Half-blocks uploads nothing.** Its encode resolves pixels into `▀`/`▄` cells, and its cost is proportional to the CELL COUNT, which the terminal has already bounded by existing. There is no 9 MB payload to avoid, so the budget is defending a expense this backend cannot incur.

## Shape of the fix

Make the cap protocol-aware rather than raising it globally: keep it for kitty/sixel/iTerm2, where the encoded-byte budget is real, and let half-blocks scale to the pane. Two things to get right:

  - **The pixel lock (SQ-0936) still applies.** `v6_fit_source` already consults `lock` before this cap, and a locked scale is always <= the free one, so the interaction is already ordered correctly — but scopa and fmvpoker are exactly the titles SQ-0936's note says fall through to this arm even in HYBRID mode, so re-check both with the lock on and off.
  - **Do not just delete `MAX_V6_UPSCALE`.** SQ-0479 doubled the native canvas 320x200 -> 640x400 and the constant was moved from 4.0 to 2.0 to keep the SAME 1280x800 output ceiling; the number encodes that history, and a reader who sees `2.0` without it will think the cap was tightened.

## Worth measuring first

Whether the halfblocks encode really is cheap at, say, 640x200 cells — that is 128,000 cells to resolve every frame, and `resize_exact` down from the composite is the real work. Cheap compared with a PNG, not free. Measure before promising the cap can go entirely; a much HIGHER cap for this backend may be the honest answer rather than none.
