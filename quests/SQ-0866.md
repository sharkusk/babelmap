MEASURED, with the real parser (temporary blorb example, since deleted):

`MountedDisk::pictures()` on `Arthur Quest 4 Excalibur.2mg` offers **nothing** — `InfocomPics::parse` rejects every file on the volume with `UnsupportedContainer` (all five `ARTHUR.D*` segments, `INFOCOM`, `INFOCOM.SYSTEM`, `INFODOS`, `PRODOS`, `BASIC.SYSTEM`, `STARTUP`). So no misparsed archive is involved, and the artwork is NOT coming off the image even though the story is.

The default option — "Use this story's own art (Blorb / disk image)" — therefore falls through to the resource Blorb beside the story. `stories/Arthur.blb` has a resource index of **326 Pict entries**. The Apple IIgs release carries **168** pictures (51/26/53/38 across segments 2–5, per SQ-0852). The loose archives are equally wrong: `arthur.pic` 169, `arthur.mg1` 171, `arthur.cg1` 170.

So r63/s890622's picture numbers are being resolved against a 326-picture DOS/Blorb set. That is the corruption the user sees, and it is the project's own rule being violated by the fallback: a disk image is a different release.

The fix is about what the fallback is ALLOWED to do, not about decoding. Two parts worth separating:
1. Art from a neighbouring file must not be applied to a disk-mounted story whose release it does not match. We know the story's release and serial; a Blorb carries an IFID; a native archive can at least be counted. Refusing a mismatch is better than drawing nonsense.
2. When nothing correct is available, drawing NO art is the honest outcome — the game is playable without pictures, and SQ-0863 is what will eventually supply the right ones.

Scheduling note: this collides with SQ-0865 (launch-options labelling, running now) over the art-resolution path, so it goes the moment that lands.
