## The gap, stated exactly

Hybrid's story viewport is CELLS ONLY. Step 29 of the pipeline (`render_node(&story.node, …, viewport, …)`, `screen.rs`) renders the transcript as terminal text and nothing else. There is no way to put a pixel inside that box.

That single fact is why `picture_takeover` exists. Its four hatches (`screen.rs:4035-4040`) are four spellings of one sentence — *"there are pixels here the ring has nowhere to put"* — and their answer is to hand the entire frame to the raster composite:

    story_covers_screen && (art_paints_anything || art_fills_screen || art_encloses_screen)
    || story_plate_escapes_story_window

## Why this is the LAST capability gap, not one of several

MEASURED during SQ-0894 (2026-08-15): hybrid already handles text-over-art everywhere EXCEPT inside the story box. Zork Zero's banner — text printed onto the ribbon artwork — is classified as an Art strip by `over_art` and rasterised into the band image, and that works on every backend including half-blocks, where a "band image" is drawn as `▀` cells.

So the often-repeated claim that raster must survive for protocol reasons is WRONG, and I made it myself earlier in SQ-0894 before checking. Half-blocks and sixel cannot show a glyph and artwork in the same CELL — but hybrid never asks them to. It rasterises the strip. The capability is already there; it is simply unavailable in one region.

**Give the story viewport the same treatment the chrome bands already get, and "raster mode" becomes "hybrid whose story region happens to be one full-screen Art strip".**

## The governing rule is already written, and it is not "is this pixel-positioned"

SQ-0892's third note settles the criterion, from the user: **"is there art beneath this text?"** — not "is this text pixel-positioned". A glyph in a terminal cell carries its own background, which occludes artwork beneath it; that is a physical fact and not something to engineer around.

| beneath the text | correct treatment |
|---|---|
| the machine's plain ground | glyphs — the runs account for every pixel |
| artwork (Zork Zero's ribbon) | raster — no glyph can reproduce what is under it |

`over_art` (`screen.rs`, via `region_has_opaque`) is the existing predicate and already decides this correctly for chrome. The work is applying it to the story region and rasterising only where it answers yes — NOT rasterising the whole viewport whenever any art is present.

## What to be careful of

- **Do not rasterise more than the art demands.** SQ-0750 is the standard: *"reserve raster for pixels the runs cannot account for"*. A story region with a picture in one corner should keep crisp glyphs everywhere else. The failure mode is a frame that reads as "raster mode by accident".
- **Scrolling.** The chrome ring is static per frame; the story region scrolls. An art strip inside the viewport has to decide whether it scrolls with the prose (a margin float — SQ-0888's ship does this) or is pinned to the window (a backdrop). Both exist in the corpus and they are not the same mechanism.
- **The transcript already has an inline-image path** (`transcript_images`, margin floats, `ImageAlign::MarginLeft/Right`). Check whether the answer is to route more through it rather than to build a second mechanism beside it. SQ-0888 routed Shogun's Apple-press ship into the text stream precisely because pinned placement was the wrong shape.
- **Measure `story_clear_native` again afterwards.** SQ-0894 measured it as a no-op across the corpus (56 frames, zero differences) — but only over frames that REACH the ring. If this quest lets frames with art behind the prose reach it, the inset finally has something to find, and step (b) of the user's ordering becomes live for real.

## Acceptance

The four `picture_takeover` hatches have somewhere to send their pixels. That does not mean removing them — SQ-0897 owns that, one at a time by measurement.

Verify on the frames that motivate it: Zork Zero's `map` command (window 0 grows to the full screen with the map painted beneath — the `art_fills_screen` case, documented at `screen.rs:3868-3880`), fmvpoker's bet panel (`story_plate_escapes_story_window`, SQ-0739), and Shogun's Amiga credits screen (`James Clavell's Shogun.adf`, r295 — window 0 spans all 640px at the menu).
