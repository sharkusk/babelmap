--- note 2026-08-09T23:33:59Z ---

User's report 2026-08-09, Arthur booted from the Amiga disk image:

> "in amiga arthur, in hybrid mode a nice border frame is being drawn, but it doesn't scale to our window width (raster mode looks great). also, hybrid mouse click not accurate (if you click way above the menu you will get a hit)"

UNIFYING HYPOTHESIS — these are probably ONE defect seen twice, and that is why they are filed together. The hybrid ring maps native game pixels onto the pane through a scale-and-offset, and the click map is the INVERSE of that same transform. If the transform is wrong, the frame is drawn at the wrong size AND clicks land at the wrong game pixel — which is exactly a frame that doesn't fill the width plus a hit registering well above the thing you aimed at. Raster looking correct fits: it composites a full frame and does not use the ring transform at all.

Test the hypothesis directly rather than fixing two things: derive the pane→native transform the ring used for a frame, compare it against the transform the click map inverts, and see whether they agree. If they do, these are two defects and should be split.

**FIRST QUESTION, and it is the user's to answer — asked of them:** does this reproduce with `stories/Arthur.blb` (the ordinary Blorb path, no disk image)? That separates a regression introduced by the interpreter profile from a pre-existing hybrid-ring defect that the Amiga route merely made visible. Do not start until that is known; the two answers point at completely different code.

PRIOR ART, related but distinct — read before theorising:
- **SQ-0706 defect A** recorded the same *shape* for scopa: clicks worked in raster and not in hybrid, diagnosed then as "the pane→native-pixel transform for the hybrid ring/letterbox must be landing clicks somewhere the game does not recognise. `V6ClickMap::map_click` and the hybrid scale/offset are where to look."
- **SQ-0738**'s agent found fmvpoker's click failure was NOT a click-map problem — both modes took the same path and recorded a byte-identical map (`img_x 16, img_w 768, img_h 480, native 640x400`, an exact inverse of the letterbox). So the click map is demonstrably correct on the takeover path. Arthur in hybrid takes the RING path instead, which is the one never cleared.
- **SQ-0563** (cell-granular clicks make sub-cell buttons unreachable) is a different, narrower defect; Arthur's menu items are large.

Corpus safety when a fix comes: the ring transform is shared by every v6 game that keeps a viewport — Zork Zero above all. Its frame-by-frame render pins are the guard.

--- note 2026-08-09T23:43:49Z ---

KEY MECHANISM, from the user 2026-08-09 — and it undercuts the one-transform hypothesis recorded above:

> "journey.z6 uses reversed space to draw borders (with ibmpc). the amiga interpreter switches those to line drawing."

**The border is TEXT, not artwork.** Under IbmPc the game draws it with reverse-video spaces; under Amiga it switches to line-drawing characters. Either way the frame is character cells the game printed, not a picture the hybrid ring composited.

So symptom 1 ("doesn't scale to our window width") is probably NOT a ring transform defect at all. It looks like the SQ-0727 family: the game draws its border against its own 640px screen at its own column count, and hybrid renders those runs as terminal cells in a pane with a different number of columns, so the border stops short. Raster looks right for the matching reason — it rasterises the runs at the game's own pixel positions into the 640x400 composite and scales the whole frame, preserving proportion.

CONSEQUENCES:
- The two symptoms may be **separate defects that merely appeared together**. The click inaccuracy can still be a ring/letterbox inversion (the path scopa was on in SQ-0706, and the one SQ-0738 never cleared); the border is text placement. Decide on evidence, and split this quest if they diverge.
- The IbmPc-vs-Amiga comparison is now worth more, for a new reason: **the game emits different characters under the two profiles.** If the border misfits only under Amiga, suspect the metrics of the glyphs it switched to — ZMSD's character-graphics font (font 3) has its own metrics, and a font selected specifically for drawing borders is exactly what a cell path can get wrong in width.
- A fix that stretches or repeats a text border to the pane width is a placement rule with corpus reach, not a Journey tweak. Zork Zero and Shogun both paint chrome text through the same cell paths.

**REVISED UNIFIED THEORY — the two symptoms share a cause after all, but not the one first proposed.** Under the Amiga profile, Journey's TEXT RUNS are drawn at the wrong positions. That one fault yields both:
- the border (which is text — reverse-video spaces under IbmPc, line-drawing glyphs under Amiga) stops short of the pane edge; and
- clicking a menu item where it is drawn misses, because the drawn position no longer matches the game's hit test.

It also explains the user's oddest detail precisely — *"if you click way above the menu you will get a hit"*. If the drawn menu sits BELOW the game's real hit boxes, clicking above the drawn menu lands on them. That is exactly SQ-0738's shape (fmvpoker's labels drawn one text row above their hit band, so the drawn row was inert and the empty row below it worked), in the opposite direction.

WHAT TO MEASURE, in order:
1. **The glyphs.** The game switches to line-drawing characters under Amiga. If they come from ZMSD's character-graphics font (font 3), its metrics differ from the normal font, and a run measured at the wrong width lands wrong and stops short.
2. **Font cell metrics carried by the profile** — anything changing pixels-per-cell moves placement and the click inverse together.
3. **Screen / standard-window geometry per profile.** Journey.blb declares a `Reso`, so this should not differ; verify rather than assume.

The concrete first measurement: render the same Journey frame under both profiles, dump each text run's declared native position alongside the cell it was drawn at, and diff. Whatever moved is the defect.

--- note 2026-08-09T23:45:58Z ---

SCREENSHOT ANALYSIS 2026-08-09 (user-supplied, Journey hybrid under the Amiga profile). Measurements approximate, from a 723px-wide capture, but the shape is unambiguous.

**The border and the transcript disagree about how wide the screen is.**
- The outer line-drawing box spans roughly x 13..650, and so does the bottom panel with its "The Party" / "Individual Commands" sub-boxes.
- The transcript text runs to roughly x 710 — PAST the border's right edge.
- The border's vertical rule visibly cuts THROUGH the prose: `"...seems; an | there"` and `"a few who can recall those years, a d| fewer"`, with the box rule sitting between a word and its continuation.

Ratio 637/697 ≈ 0.91, about what you get if the border lands at the game's 80 columns while the transcript wraps at ~87. So the frame is not "too small" in isolation; the two disagree.

**WHY IbmPc LOOKS FINE — hypothesis, and it points at code changed today.** Under IbmPc the border is reverse-video SPACES, i.e. runs with an explicit background, and `fill_explicit_bg_rows` pads such a row out to its full window width (SQ-0519, narrowed by SQ-0721 to "a window is a bar only when its runs reach both of its edges"). An IbmPc border therefore gets stretched to the window edge automatically and looks correct **by accident of that rule**. Under Amiga the border is line-drawing GLYPHS — discrete characters with no background band — so nothing stretches them and they land at their literal column count while the prose wraps wider.

**IF THAT HOLDS, THE DEFECT IS NOT THE BORDER.** It is that **the transcript wraps to the pane width instead of the game's declared story-window width**, and IbmPc merely conceals it because its border stretches to meet the prose. The fix would then belong on the transcript side, and SQ-0697 is the adjacent work — it made a story window's box PLACE its transcript without also constraining it to the box's WIDTH.

This also bears on the clicks: if the transcript uses a width the game did not declare, mapping a clicked cell back to a game pixel through that width lands off — consistent with "click above the drawn menu and you get a hit".

TO VERIFY, not adopt: dump the story window's declared box, the pane's column count, and the column at which the transcript actually wraps, under both profiles. If the wrap column tracks the pane rather than the box, that is it.

CORPUS CAUTION, now sharper: the transcript wrap width is shared by every v6 game — Zork Zero, Shogun and Arthur all wrap prose in a viewport. Their frames must stay byte-identical.
