--- note 2026-07-16T02:05:54Z ---

DEFERRED after a prototype + measurement pass. Nothing landed; the tree was reverted to d495788/67f0dc4 (mapper 204, app 1417 green). Findings below are the value of the session — they are measured, not guessed, and several contradict what I predicted.

FEASIBILITY: the renderer already has the branch. `pure_diagonal` in plot_connector draws a connector as ONE corner-to-corner chain; it is merely gated on `pix.len() == 3` (diagonally ADJACENT rooms). Dropping that gate is mechanically the whole feature.

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE (real Zork map, full before/after dumped and eyeballed): 9 rows of 90 change; total ink 1970 -> 1971 cells (+1); half-diagonal glyphs 30 -> 35. It REMOVES long horizontal runs rather than adding sprawl — the chain replaces the L it would otherwise draw. My initial "it sprawls / costs more ink" claim was wrong: the per-connector count (38 chain cells vs 30 orth) ignored that the orth run is deleted.

BLOCKER THAT KILLED IT — merge stubs (user spotted this, not me):
`143 -W-> 68` is a MERGE STUB (merge=true, points=[(2,4),(1,4),(1,5)]). An extra edge between an already-connected pair does not draw its own line home; it routes to the TRUNK and T-junctions onto it, deliberately ENDING at the trunk's points[len-2]. It only looks complete because the trunk draws an orthogonal body through that junction. Go direct and the trunk's body vanishes -> the stub terminates in mid-air (a dangling `│`). Merge stubs are not self-sufficient; they BORROW the trunk's line. Zork: 3 merge stubs, 1 on a diagonal trunk.
Chosen resolution (user picked option 2 of 4): the stub should T-junction onto the CHAIN. Encouraging: the stub already REACHES the chain and even renders a correct `┼` there via SQ-0356's merge — it just OVERSHOOTS by one cell. Truncating it at the first chain cell turns its mask from N|S into N, which merges with the chain's E|W into `┴`, a real T-junction, using the rule already shipped. Not attempted.

SECOND GAP: the chain crosses `┊` up/down portal lines. SQ-0356's merge is scoped to compass connectors (up/down keep a separate dotted mask), on the then-true evidence that a chain never met one. Direct diagonals falsify that immediately -> a visible gap at every `┊` crossing. Fix is the same merge rule widened to the dotted set.

THE ROUTER GATE (built, tested, mutation-verified, then reverted — reconstructible from this note):
Needed because `pix.len() == 3` only INCIDENTALLY guaranteed clearance (no room between adjacent rooms). Added `RoutedConnector.direct_diagonal: bool`, set at the lattice-route construction site as `arrive.is_some() && diagonal_clear(a, b, c.dir, &occupied)`; false at the merge-stub and direct_route sites (the latter is only reached when !corner_ended, so never a diagonal).
`diagonal_clear(a, b, dir, occupied)`:
  - requires `b` in `dir`'s own quadrant (the chain steps that way by construction; a distorted "diagonal" would be drawn walking AWAY from its target);
  - the geometry MUST be done in doubled coords, not cell coords. A diagonal leaves the box CORNER, so it travels the GAPS between cells, not their centres. Both corners of `68 -NE-> 143` sit on doubled row 5 (odd = the channel above the row), so it clears "Living Room" entirely — a centre-to-centre cell line calls it blocked. This is the crux of the whole test.
  - corners are odd/odd, so `(x+1)/2` (exact — numerator always even) re-expresses them in BOX units, where box (c,r) is the OPEN unit square (c,c+1)x(r,r+1) and every corner is an integer lattice point. `u1==u2 || v1==v2` => runs along a gap line => touches no box interior at all (also covers adjacent diagonals, whose corners coincide). Otherwise a slab test per candidate box; a box merely CLIPPED at a corner gives a zero-width parameter window and does not block.
  - `a`/`b` need no exclusion: a corner is one step out on BOTH axes, so in box units it lands exactly on its own box's boundary, putting both endpoints' boxes outside the loop range. (Proven by mutation: removing the guard changed nothing across 209 tests — it was dead code.)
Result on the real map: all 4 layer-0 diagonals direct=true, plus 2 on layer 1; no non-diagonal ever direct. Mutants died: dropping the quadrant check, and ignoring `occupied`, each failed a test.

THE GENERALISATION (user asked; measured, not attempted): "let all paths take the shortest path including diagonals". `diagonal_chain(anchor, target, dir, g)` takes ANY anchor — the box corner was SQ-0314's choice, not a constraint — so a cardinal edge could leave its side (keeping its arrow, so direction still reads) and then chain, with `dir` from the sign of the offset rather than the compass label.
I predicted the lane system was a structural blocker (diagonals carry no lane, so nothing separates them). MEASUREMENT SAYS NO: chaining all 27 connectors gives 14 chained / 13 axis-aligned, 637 chain cells, and only ONE cell wanted by 2+ chains (worst contention 2). Diagonals barely collide — they spend their length in territory orthogonal routes never enter. The real obstacle is 2 of 14 chains ploughing through a room box, which is exactly what `diagonal_clear` catches. Caveat: that count is chain-vs-chain only; chain-vs-orthogonal-run from the 13 straight connectors was not measured.

WHY DEFERRED: the feature is one line; making it CORRECT is merge-stub re-anchoring + up/down merge + the router gate, i.e. three interlocking changes across the router/renderer boundary. Related standing gap: overlap_stats/tidy score the orthogonal reading only (see SQ-0356), so tidy cannot see chain collisions at all.
