## Why it surfaced now

SQ-0448 gave the Memory view a caption under its `addr:` line — `dict word: "lantern"`, `object 27 name: "brass lantern"` — so you can confirm you landed on the entry you meant. An object short-name can be long ("a small piece of vitreous slag"), and it sits under a hex dump that is already wide: an address column, 16 hex bytes, and a char-preview column. At most pane widths the row is cut off, and so is the caption, which defeats the point of decoding it.

So this is not only about the caption. The hex rows themselves have been clipped all along; the caption is what made it obvious.

## Worth deciding rather than assuming

  - **Scroll, or reflow?** A memory dump is conventionally 16 bytes per row and a reader counts on that — reflowing to 8 at a narrow width changes every address in the left column and makes two sessions hard to compare. Horizontal scrolling keeps the grid honest. The user asked for scrolling; this note records why that is the right instinct rather than merely the stated one.
  - **What scrolls?** The dump and the caption may want different behaviour: the caption is prose and could ellipsise or wrap, while the dump is a grid that should slide. Two mechanisms in one pane is worse than one unless the difference earns it.
  - **Keys.** There is a standing convention worth honouring: Shift-Tab reverses any Tab-cycler, and shared-chrome dialogs do Tab/Shift-Tab focus, Enter activate, Esc cancel, with Space widget-reserved. Whatever binding is chosen, it goes in the `slash::COMMANDS` registry rather than being hardwired — keys bind to command strings.
  - **Styleability.** Any new indicator (a scroll marker, a truncation ellipsis) needs a `ColorScheme` field, a `style.rs` selector and a render apply. SQ-0448 added `debug.zstring` this way and found that `style.example.toml` is GENERATED and its check test fails until the new selector is listed there — worth knowing before starting.

## Scope note

The Memory view is the case in hand, but check the neighbouring debug panes (disassembly, inspector) before building something that only one of them can use. If two of them want it, the mechanism belongs where they share it.
