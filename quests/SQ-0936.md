## Design settled in conversation, 2026-08-20 — ready to build from

### The ladder is DERIVED, not chosen

What has to land on whole device pixels is the ART pixel, not the unit-screen
pixel — that is where nearest-neighbour crispness lives. An art pixel is
`art_scale` unit pixels and a unit pixel is `s` device pixels, so both axes must
satisfy `art_scale.N · s ∈ ℤ`, which gives

    step = 1 / gcd(art_scale.0, art_scale.1)

`PictSource::art_scale` is already a PER-AXIS pair, computed at boot from the
archive's own declared picture space, so nothing new has to be measured:

    press                    art space   art_scale  gcd  step   ladder
    most v6 (Blorb/Amiga/DOS) 320x200     (2, 2)     2    1/2    0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 …
    Macintosh mono Pic.data   480x300     (1, 1)     1    1      1, 2, 3 …
    Macintosh colour CPic     320x200     (2, 2)     2    1/2    half-steps
    EGA                       640x200     (1, 2)     1    1      1, 2, 3 …
    Apple II                  140x192     (4, 2)     2    1/2    half-steps

So the user's "1x, 1.5x, 2x, 2.5x" intuition is right for the common case and
FALLS OUT rather than being assumed — and the Macintosh and EGA differ for a
reason the code can compute. Hardcoding one ladder everywhere would be wrong for
both.

### H and V: one magnification, then vertical tiling

The magnification stays UNIFORM (H = V) and is applied to the art BEFORE any
tiling; tiling is a vertical-only operation in the already-scaled space. This is
the pipeline SQ-0698 established — "every band's horizontal factor is the uniform
letterbox scale, which is the top plate's factor", while vertically Arthur repeats
a strip under a foot, Shogun stamps a flipped second copy, and Zork Zero runs cap /
tiled shaft / foot.

**A consequence worth having: quantising the magnification makes the tile height
integral for free.** A tile is cut from art at whole art-pixel boundaries, so if one
art pixel is a whole number of device pixels then so is the tile, and every repeat
lands on an exact boundary with no sub-pixel creep down a tall flank. Crisp art and
seamless tiling come from the same constraint; there is no second knob.

Per-axis non-squareness (EGA, Apple II) is already carried by `art_scale`, so the
uniform `s` on top preserves aspect and nothing else needs to know about it.

### One GLOBAL scale, and Journey is what settles that

There were two readings of "quantised scaling": (a) the one global letterbox scale
for the whole 640x400 screen, or (b) per-picture inside whatever window the game
drew. The ring games cannot distinguish them — their art IS the frame and fills the
screen. **Journey can**: its picture sits in its own window with a drawn divider
beside it, so under (b) the art would stop short of its own frame and a gap would
open between picture and rule — the defect SQ-0698's "picture column unstretched,
extend only the divider" was about. Under (a) the window rect and the picture scale
together and the art still fills it exactly.

So: (a), one global factor, and Journey is lumped in with the rest — its frame
letterboxes horizontally rather than spanning the pane, which the user has
explicitly accepted as the simpler behaviour.

**The margin is not dead space.** `fill_pane_page` paints the whole pane edge to
edge with the story's page (SQ-0532 wave-5), and since SQ-0935 a game that names no
page gets the MACHINE's there. A wider margin is more of the machine's own screen.

### The one real conflict to resolve while building

`stretch_flanks` reclaims letterbox slack on the `Frame` and `Menu` plans — which
is Arthur, Shogun and Zork Zero, exactly the games this mode is for. Quantising
DOWN leaves more slack, the reclaim stretches the flanks further to fill it, and a
stretched flank is no longer at the quantised magnification. That would buy crisp
art and then stretch it away on the three titles that matter most.

Given SQ-0698 already moved side borders from stretching to tiling for the same
class of reason, the consistent answer is that in quantised mode the remainder is
TILED, not stretched: the flank repeats once more and clips, keeping the factor
intact.

NOT YET VERIFIED, and it decides whether the conflict is real:
`draw_chrome_band_stretched` still exists and is still selected for `Frame`/`Menu`.
Look at a real frame and see whether that path stretches ART or only extends a
plain-coloured region. The first conflicts with quantisation; the second does not.

### Shape

A MODE, not a default — free scaling uses the pane better and someone on a small
terminal will want it. `uniform_scale` already centres (`off_x`/`off_y`), so the
change is: quantise `s` down to the nearest multiple of `step`, plus a config key
and a `style.toml`-free settings row. Needs a floor for a pane too small for the
smallest step — fall back to free scaling rather than clip, probably; there is
precedent for bounds in `MAX_V6_UPSCALE`.

## CORRECTION to the note above: the stretch/tile conflict is not real

The previous note flagged an open conflict — that `stretch_flanks` reclaiming
letterbox slack on the `Frame`/`Menu` plans would stretch the side borders and undo
the quantised magnification. Checked, and it does not.

`draw_chrome_band_stretched` has exactly two callers in `render/screen.rs`:

  - `BandSlot::DividerExtension` — running a divider RULE further down. A rule is a
    solid column of one colour, so stretching it cannot blur anything.
  - `BandSlot::Art` — a flank PANEL fill (`fill_pane_page` then a crop into the
    panel), which the comment describes as being there "instead of top-anchoring the
    art over bare backdrop".

The ornate side borders do not go through it at all. Since SQ-0698 they are TILED —
Arthur repeats a strip under a foot, Shogun stamps a flipped second copy, Zork Zero
runs cap / tiled shaft / foot — and tiling repeats at whatever magnification the
tile already carries. So more leftover space means one more REPEAT and a longer
rule, both of which preserve the quantised factor rather than fighting it.

Conclusion: quantising the scale down is safe on the very games the mode is for,
and no "tile the remainder instead of stretching it" work is needed — that is
already what happens.

Caveat, since this is from reading the call sites rather than measuring: the
DividerExtension case is unambiguous, the flank-panel one less so, because a panel
fill could in principle carry art. Glance at a real Arthur or Shogun frame during
implementation and confirm the panel path is a fill rather than ornate artwork.

With that resolved the quest has no open design questions left — only the floor
behaviour for a pane too small for the smallest step, which is a choice rather than
an unknown.

## Two gaps found while reviewing the previews (2026-08-20), both blocking "done"

The implementation on `worktree-agent-ae4e1febeba6d5f92` (`d2691ad3`) works on the
games that take the hybrid RING — Zork Zero, Shogun, Arthur, Journey and the
Macintosh press all visibly drop a rung and gain page margin. Gate on that branch
is 5724/5724 with the full fixture set (`stories/` + `treasures/` + `masterpieces/`
symlinked; a branch report of one failure was an artifact of only `stories/` being
linked, and `native_disk_sound::the_pitch_model_reproduces_both_blorbs` passes on
main).

### 1. Titles that fall through to the raster composite get NOTHING — in hybrid mode

Measured with `pty_capture`, seed pinned via `random_seed` so the deal is
identical, comparing lock off vs on at 144x40:

    scopa      0 differing pixels
    fmvpoker   0 differing pixels

(A first fmvpoker run showed 37,097 differing pixels and was pure card randomness,
not scaling. Pin `random_seed` in both config dirs or the comparison is worthless.)

The oracle says why: each emits ONE full-frame placement, `rows 1..37 cols 5..138`,
rather than the ring's separate bands. That is the raster composite, whose scale
comes from `ratatui_image`'s own fit inside `spawn_v6_encode` and never touches
`uniform_scale`, which is the only thing `v6_pixel_lock` gates.

**This is not the "setting does nothing in `v6_render = raster`" caveat.** Both were
captured in the DEFAULT hybrid mode. They publish no primary Buffer — scopa's screen
is three Grids drawn with `erase_window` fills (SQ-0711) — so hybrid falls through
to the raster arm for them regardless of the configured mode. Two v6 titles in the
corpus therefore never see the feature, and they are arguably the ones that want it
most: hard-edged UI art, buttons and card faces, where fractional scaling shows
worst. The user identified it by eye from the font rendering before the measurement
confirmed it.

### 2. And wiring the raster arm alone would still get scopa WRONG

`art_scale` defaults to `(V6_ART_SCALE, V6_ART_SCALE)` = (2,2) when an archive
declares no picture space. That is right for every Infocom blorb, which all declare
a 320x200 `Reso` and are doubled. It is wrong for scopa: `scopa.blb` declares
nothing precisely BECAUSE its card art is already drawn for the 640x400 screen —
the finding the v6-graphics doc already records, where doubling it told the game its
cards were 104x168 and its sample cards overlapped and hung off the bottom.

So scopa wants step 1, and the default hands it step 1/2 — locking 1:1 art onto 1.5
device pixels, exactly the blur this quest exists to remove. The signal is already
available and already used: absence of a `Reso` chunk is what the existing doubling
decision keys on. The ladder needs to read the same thing rather than taking the
field's default.

### Suggested shape

Fix 2 first and independently — it is a correctness bug in the ladder's input and is
testable without touching the render path. Then decide 1: either gate the raster
arm's fit on the same locked scale, or accept the limitation and say so in the
settings row and the docs, which currently imply it applies to v6 generally.
