Lead on item 2 (F-key emits a line feed at the cursor), not yet confirmed: a listed terminator goes through `main.rs`'s `plain` branch → `submit_line_with_terminator(&cmd, term)` → `turn::finish_command_turn(&cmd, …)`, and `cmd` is EMPTY when the player pressed a function key without typing anything. `finish_command_turn` then still runs its echo block: with `command_bar` on, or when the last transcript line is not story, it does `push_transcript_kind("> ")` — a bare prompt line with nothing after it, which is exactly a stray line feed at the cursor. The echo exists to show what the player typed; with nothing typed there is nothing to echo. Suspect the fix is to skip the echo when the line was terminated by a special key and the command is empty, but do NOT conflate that with a plain Enter on an empty line, which legitimately echoes (that submit goes through a different path).

Verify before fixing: ZMSD §7.1.1.1 on whether the interpreter echoes the terminating newline when the read ended on a terminating character other than 13 — read the standard, do not take the above on trust. Frotz's `read_line` is the cross-check.

--- note 2026-08-15T04:15:42Z ---

ITEM 3 ROOT CAUSE FOUND (not yet fixed). `PictSource::art_scale` in crates/app/src/graphics.rs:627 divides with INTEGER division:

    let unit_w = INFOCOM_V6_STD_WINDOW.0 * V6_ART_SCALE;   // 320 * 2 = 640
    let unit_h = INFOCOM_V6_STD_WINDOW.1 * V6_ART_SCALE;   // 200 * 2 = 400
    Some(((unit_w / space_w).max(1), (unit_h / space_h).max(1)))

Every archive in the corpus until now had a picture space that DIVIDES 640x400: 320-wide gives scale 2, 640-wide gives scale 1. The Macintosh monochrome archive is 480x300 — the first that does not — and 640/480 truncates to 1, 400/300 truncates to 1. So the art is not scaled at all and the unit screen becomes 480x304 rather than the fixed 640x400 presentation space `session.rs`'s own docs promise ("the unit screen is the same 640x400 for every rendition — it is babelmap's presentation space, not the card's"). The 304 is 300 rounded up to a whole 16-pixel row grid (19 x 16).

Verified with `cargo run -p app --example border_preview`, which prints the geometry outright:
  CPIC.DATA: picture space 320x200 at scale 2x2 -> art screen 640x400, native screen 640x400
  PIC.DATA:  picture space 480x300 at scale 1x1 -> art screen 480x300, native screen 480x304

The same preview shows both flanks COMPOSING correctly (colour 12 units wide over 368 art rows, mono 8 units wide over 275), so the flank extraction and the border recognition are sound — the reported corruption is downstream placement, which is consistent with the game being told a 480x304 screen while the surrounding layout expects 640x400. The status/score bar symptom fits the same cause: Arthur's bar is pixel-positioned runs mapped to a native row, and the row grid moved.

STATED AS A LEAD, NOT A PROOF: the truncation is certain and it is exactly the archive reported broken, but I have not rendered the full screen to confirm it produces the specific artefacts described. Do that first.

Fix is a real design decision, not a one-liner — `art_scale` returns (u32, u32) and cannot express 4/3. Either it gains a rational/float scale (and every consumer is checked), or the unit screen is allowed to be per-archive and the layout is made to follow it consistently. Check `INFOCOM_V6_STD_WINDOW` too: session.rs already carries a third copy of (320, 200), noted as a follow-up in an earlier sweep.

--- note 2026-08-15T04:21:11Z ---

ITEM 3, third and decisive piece — the user identified the artefact precisely: the bottom of the Macintosh B/W flank is a piece of the UPPER BANNER, tiled. Confirmed visually with `cargo run -p app --example border_preview` (contact sheets and 8x magnified crops); the flank COMPOSES and the border is recognised, so extraction is sound. Measured periodicity: the mono flank repeats on a 48-row period over 71% of rows, the colour one on 64 over 86% — both in the art region AND the extension, so the tiling itself is working; what is wrong is WHICH strip is being tiled.

All three findings are one root cause. `render/v6_border.rs::arthur()` reads:

    /// Bocfel's `pillar_height` argument is 2 raw lines; unit space doubles it.
    const UNIT_ROWS: u32 = 4;
    let top_cut = (art_bottom * CUT_NUM / CUT_DEN) & !1;   // 90%, rounded to even
    extend_pillars(dst, top_cut, foot_height, art_bottom, UNIT_ROWS, 0, false, desired_height);

"unit space doubles it" IS `V6_ART_SCALE = 2` — the 320-wide archive's scale — hard-coded as a constant. The Macintosh monochrome archive is 480x300 and `PictSource::art_scale` gives it (1,1) (by integer truncation, 640/480 = 1), so unit space does NOT double there: its repeat unit should be 2 rows, not 4. `top_cut`'s 90%-and-round-to-even is likewise computed against a differently scaled `art_bottom`, so the 4-row strip is cut from the wrong place and what gets tiled down the pane is banner rather than shaft.

The `& !1` even-rounding is itself a phase guard that only makes sense at scale 2 ("so the doubled texture keeps its phase" — its own comment).

So the whole of item 3 is: babelmap assumes every v6 picture space is 320x200 doubled into a 640x400 unit screen. Three places encode that assumption independently — `art_scale`'s integer division, `session::V6_ART_SCALE`, and `arthur()`'s UNIT_ROWS/top_cut — and the Macintosh B/W archive is the first medium in the corpus that is neither 320 nor 640 wide. Also note the poles are documented as "a plain vertical texture", which is true of the Amiga/colour art and NOT of the Mac B/W art, which is a dithered texture with ornaments — so a mis-cut strip is far more visible there.

DO NOT fix this piecemeal. `v6_frame_border_medium.rs` states the standing rule: a side column that is genuine artwork staying a bitmap matters more than the bug, and "getting that wrong is a worse regression". The fix needs the scale to become a real per-archive quantity that `arthur()`, `shogun()` and `zork_zero()` all read, with the border sweep re-run across the corpus (`v6_archive_border_sweep.rs`) and the preview eyeballed for every title before and after.

Item 1 (DOS Arthur F2 map screen grey ground, rainbow side scrolls) is still untouched.

CORRECTION to the previous note on item 3 — my `arthur()`/UNIT_ROWS diagnosis was WRONG and the user caught it. Two reasons it cannot be right: `arthur()` cuts its strip at 90% of the pole height, so the tiled texture comes from near the BOTTOM and can never reproduce the banner; and the 48-row periodicity I cited as corroboration is present in the ART REGION itself (0.79 at period 48 over rows 0..275), so it describes the source texture and says nothing about the tiling. A 4-row tile cannot produce a 48-row period. I read a measurement as support when it was irrelevant.

THE ACTUAL MECHANISM. `render/v6_border.rs::recognize` distinguishes the three border shapes like this:

    if bottom + V6_TEXT_ROW > native_h && lo * 10 < hi * 9 { ZorkZeroPillars }
    if top == 0 { ShogunSinglePiece }
    ArthurPoles

Arthur is told from Shogun by ONE thing: whether the flank art starts at native row 0. `arthur()`'s own doc records the measurement it was built on — "Arthur - The Quest for Excalibur.adf (release 54, serial 890606): the poles run native rows 11..379" — so the Amiga press has top = 11 and lands on ArthurPoles.

The Macintosh press does not. border_preview reports `art 0..368` for CPIC.DATA and `art 0..275` for PIC.DATA: BOTH start at row 0, so both are classified ShogunSinglePiece, and `shogun()` extends by stamping a whole second copy of the border below the first — banner included. That is precisely the artefact reported.

Why only the B/W one shows it: `flank_source` returns None when `desired <= art.1`. The colour flank fills 368 of its 400 unit rows and mostly never extends; the monochrome one fills 275 (of a 304-row unit screen) and does, so the second copy actually gets stamped.

The `art_scale` integer-truncation finding (480x300 giving scale 1x1 and a 480x304 unit screen instead of 640x400) STANDS as a separate real defect, and it is very likely what makes the mono art short relative to its pane and so triggers the extension at all. But it is not what draws the banner.

WHAT TO FIX, and the order: the classifier is the bug. `top == 0` is a proxy for "single-piece border" that happens to hold on the Amiga corpus and fails on the Macintosh press of the same game. A shape test that cannot tell two titles apart when one of them ships its art flush to the top needs a real discriminator — Arthur's flank is two poles with a gap, Shogun's is one continuous piece. Do NOT simply special-case Arthur by title: nothing here writes down a picture id or a game name, by design (`border_preview`'s header states it).

Verify with `cargo run -p app --example border_preview --archive "<masterpieces bin>" --pictures "MAC/ARTHUR FOLDER/PIC.DATA" --rows 500`, and re-run `v6_archive_border_sweep.rs` across the whole corpus after any change to `recognize` — every title's classification runs through it.

1184 = 148 cells x 8px = the full screen width; 18 = ONE text row. So a frame is being uploaded as a separate full-width RGBA strip PER TEXT ROW. That is the SQ-0750 pathology ("never rasterise what the game printed as a character" — Journey's 8x900 rules, ~192 KB/frame to draw 200 box characters) at a much worse scale, and it is a plausible mechanism for the reported "the correct colour blinks quickly then shows these incorrect colours": the frame is repainted by a flood of per-row image placements.

The oracle also disagrees with our own decoder on coverage — many lines of `image cover row N cols A..B: ours image 0x…, oracle none`, i.e. we believe cells carry a placement that a real terminal does not resolve. Per SQ-0764 that is exactly what the oracle exists to catch (placement lifetime, z-order, stale placements, unicode-placeholder continuation), and it should be read before any further theory.

TWO CORRECTIONS to earlier notes, both of which sent the last session down blind alleys.

1. THE "1184x18 PER-ROW STRIPS" FINDING WAS MEASURING THE WRONG GAME. Every pty capture in the previous note used `--story <masterpieces.bin> --pictures "MAC/ARTHUR FOLDER/PIC.DATA"`. Handing babelmap a whole compilation disc launches ZORK ZERO; the archive flag names art, it does not pick a story. So the 1205 APC commands and the 44 full-width `1184x18px` RGBA strips are Zork Zero's, not Arthur's, and nothing was ever concluded from them that applies here. Real Mac Arthur uploads a 960x608 raster at the title and 64x396 / 56x396 flank tiles in play. To drive one story off a disc, extract it (`MountedDisk::read_named`) and run the file: there is no CLI flag for a disc entry.

2. `art_scale`'s "integer truncation defect" IS NOT A DEFECT. 480x300 -> scale (1,1) -> a 480x304 native screen is DELIBERATE and documented at `PictSource::native_std_window` (SQ-0838): `mac/gfx.p` calls the monochrome plate "scaled for a 480x300 screen (std Mac)" and Infocom's interpreter opened a 480x300 window for it; the uniform rule is unit space divided by picture space, and doubling it would put a 960x600 plate on a 640x400 screen. The 304 is 300 rounded up to a whole 16px row, with four pixels of slack the doc names by hand. Nothing to fix; the earlier note was wrong and should not seed another quest.

ITEM 3 — the real mechanism, and it is NOT the one the previous note named. `top == 0` never came into it: Arthur's Macintosh flank measures art rows 14..289 IN GAME (the archive-composed preview shows 0..275, which is why border_preview could not see this). It took `recognize`'s FIRST branch, ZorkZeroPillars, because SQ-0841 loosened "reaches the bottom" to one 16px text row for the 480x300-on-304 rounding, and 289 is fifteen short — one inside the slack — while the flank also narrows 16-wide to 5-wide like a pillar. So Zork Zero's masonry recipe stamped its capital down Arthur's side.

Fix: charge the rounding ONCE, against the whole inset `top + (native_h - bottom)`, not the bottom alone. Measured in-game at 165x50 through pty_capture, Zork Zero 0-4 and Shogun 0 against Arthur 29-32 — a factor of seven. `top == 0` was tried first and the sweep refused it: `Zork Zero - The Revenge of Megaboz.adf` plate 6 starts at row 2 on the right and 0 on the left, and `v6_archive_border_sweep` requires one plate's two crops to classify alike. It has to be a tolerance.

Also landed: SINGLE_PIECE_MIN_WIDTH = 24 on the Shogun branch (Shogun's narrowest flank is 44, Arthur's widest 12). That one is inert in the renderer — no Arthur flank starts at row 0 in game — but it is what the archive-composed sweep sees, where every plate starts at 0, and the `.adf` pin moves slabs -> poles because of it.

ITEM 1 — the map palette, and the user's own read was right ("the correct colour blinks quickly then shows these incorrect colours"). The direct draw is correct; SQ-0567's replay, which rebuilds a window when the Current Palette changes so Arthur's adaptive frame (54/170/171) follows the scene, was pushing EVERY picture through the live palette, base ones included. Arthur's map lays down seven pictures (137, 108, 115, 112, 138, 147, 140) and on `.MG1` those carry THREE distinct palettes — an MCGA DAC has 256 entries and Infocom used them — where the Amiga archive gives that same screen ONE. Forced through the last of the three, the parchment came back as DEFAULT_PALETTE entry 8 (grey ground) and 9/10 (rainbow rods) wherever the borrowed table ran out. The Amiga and the Blorb were never wrong because their palettes agree with each other; SQ-0880 making MCGA the default is what exposed it.

Fix: `image_under_current_palette` sends only ADAPTIVE pictures through the live palette, which is what Blorb §11.3 actually says. MCGA now matches the Blorb colour for colour.
