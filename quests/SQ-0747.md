MEASURED, not inferred. Split off SQ-0742's second pass. The user's report, Journey in hybrid under the Amiga profile:

> "the graphics on left is not honoring the frame (i.e. it is too wide). The border glyphs are slightly out of alignment at the top corner (perhaps they are from different ascii sections). ... At some widths you can see that we are rendering the left image behind the left panel (the top is peeking out)."

SQ-0742's second pass fixed the fourth item (the crooked menu columns, commit 8c9d150c) and measured these three rather than fixing them. The controlled A/B is IbmPc vs Amiga at the SAME pane, cell 8x18, honour on, headless hybrid, Journey at its party menu (`stories/journey-r83-s890706.z6`, six inputs from boot).

**(A) THE PANEL FILL OVERRUNS THE FRAME'S INNER RULE — PRE-EXISTING, IDENTICAL UNDER BOTH PROFILES.** Not "the graphics are too wide": the ILLUSTRATION is placed correctly. It is the Menu-plan flank PANEL FILL (`menu_flank_panel` → `fill_pane_page(band, bg, buf)`) that floods the entire side band, and the band always runs from the pane's left edge to the VIEWPORT's left column — which is one to two columns past the frame's inner rule.

| pane | profile | inner rule (native px → col) | picture image cells | side band cells | overrun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 96x51  | IbmPc | 233 (rev. space) → 35 | (0,0,35,20) | (0,0,**36**,45) | 1 |
| 96x51  | Amiga | 257 (`│`) → 38        | (1,1,37,18) | (0,2,**40**,42) | 2 |
| 110x51 | IbmPc | 233 → 40              | (0,0,40,23) | (0,0,**42**,44) | 2 |
| 110x51 | Amiga | 257 → 44              | (1,1,43,21) | (0,2,**46**,40) | 2 |
| 138x51 | IbmPc | 233 → 50              | (0,0,50,29) | (0,0,**52**,42) | 2 |
| 138x51 | Amiga | 257 → 55              | (2,2,53,26) | (0,2,**57**,38) | 2 |

Same structure at every width, both profiles. IbmPc hides it because its "panel" colour and its border are the same reverse-video black, so the spill is invisible; the Amiga panel is a distinct colour and its border is a thin glyph, so the fill shows to the RIGHT of the rule. **Pre-existing under the ring path, newly visible because SQ-0742 routed Journey/Amiga to the ring.** Fix direction: the flank panel fill should stop at the frame's inner rule (which `flank_divider_extension` already locates), not at the viewport.

**(B) THE TOP CORNER GLYPH IS OUT OF ALIGNMENT WITH THE VERTICAL BELOW IT — a ceil-vs-round mismatch on a shared boundary.** The user's "perhaps they are from different ascii sections" is close: they are from different SUBSYSTEMS. The top border row is a TEXT strip (crisp glyphs at `run_cell` columns, which ROUND), while the vertical rules below it come from `flank_divider_extension`, whose `dcell0`/`dcell1` FLOOR and CEIL the same native pixel. Two roundings of one boundary, so the corner and the shaft it should sit on can differ by a column. This is the exact class CLAUDE.md flags ("watch for ceil-vs-round mismatches on shared boundaries").

**(C) A LEFTOVER FULL-WIDTH ART BAND PAINTS THE PICTURE'S TOP ABOVE THE PANEL — AMIGA-ONLY, WIDTH-DEPENDENT.** `/dump-windows` strips at the same panes:

- IbmPc, 96/110/138 x51: **no full-width strip at all.**
- Amiga 96x51: `strip:text(74 runs) (0,0,96,1)` then `strip:art (0,1,96,1)` — DRAWN.
- Amiga 110x51: `strip:text(74 runs) (0,0,110,1)` then `strip:art (0,1,110,1)` — DRAWN.
- Amiga 138x51: same two, but the art band is `skipped — no art behind it`.

The flank panel starts at row 2; that one-row full-width band at row 1 rasterises a slice of the chrome canvas across the pane and lands ABOVE the panel. In a halfblock dump at 110x51 row 1 reads `▄▄ ▄▄▄▄▄…▄▄` with ink only in cols 0..44 and 108..109 — i.e. the picture's own top sliver and the right border column, sitting one row above the lavender panel that should contain them. Exactly "the top is peeking out", and exactly "at some widths" (drawn at 96 and 110, skipped at 138).

WHY AMIGA ONLY: Amiga's frame has a TEXT top border row (game row 0), so `decompose_chrome_strips` carves row 0 as Text and leaves row 1 as a separate full-width Art band above a viewport that starts at row 2. IbmPc's picture window starts at native y=0 with no top border row, the viewport starts at row 0, and no full-width band is ever produced. So this is a RING defect that Journey/Amiga is the first title to hit — not pre-existing-and-visible like (A), and not caused by the rule-collapse either.

Fix direction, deliberately NOT taken in SQ-0742 because it has corpus reach (Zork Zero and Shogun flanks go through the same decomposition): a Menu-plan flank owns its columns top to bottom, so a full-width chrome band must not paint the columns a flank panel claims. Also worth checking the `.max(0.0)` clamp in `strip_has_art`/`draw_chrome_band`: a band whose native span maps entirely above the canvas is clamped to native row 0 and drawn anyway rather than skipped, which is what makes this width-dependent.

NOT REPRODUCIBLE, recorded so it is not chased again: the reading of the screenshot that had the bottom panel's LABELS being eaten (`─The─`, `─Individual C─`) does not occur in the model. 18414 configurations were swept — cell metrics 8x18 / 10x20 / 7x15, both `honor_game_colours` modes, rows 26..56, cols 72..170, and the exact game state from the screenshot (Bergon/Praxix/Esher/Tag with "Get Advice") — and "The Party" and "Individual Commands" were intact in every one, with no menu drawn twice and no extra dividers. The only defect at those sizes was the crooked column divider, which SQ-0742 fixed. Analytically a rule cannot eat the label before it either: its left edge is the previous span's END. If the user still sees eaten labels after 8c9d150c, the missing variable is something the headless harness does not model — most likely the real kitty/sixel image compositing ABOVE the cells — and a fresh screenshot at a stated terminal size would settle it.

Result: `STATES BAD = 0`. Not one eaten label at any pane ≥48 columns in any state, including the one from the screenshot (Bergon/Praxix/Esher/Tag with "Get Advice").

**WHAT DOES EAT THEM — the exact threshold, which is well below the user's pane.** Swept cols 24..120 at 40 and 69 rows:

> `rows=40: labels intact from 48 columns upward`
> `rows=69: labels intact from 48 columns upward`

Below 48 story-pane columns the header collapses, e.g. at 28 columns `"│──────The PartyIndividual C"` and at 24 `"│─────The PartIndividual"` — which is the closest thing in the whole search space to the `─The─` / `─Individual C─` reading. But it is not overwriting: it is the game's 80-column menu not fitting, so the inter-label rules shrink to zero width and the row runs off the pane edge. **48 columns is roughly what a SPLIT layout gives at a 100-column terminal** — worth asking the user to confirm the map pane really was closed, because a split pane lands right on this threshold.

Analytically it still cannot happen at 98 columns. Overflow needs the scale below one terminal column per native 8px cell, i.e. `min(cols·cell_w/640, rows·cell_h/400) < 1`; at 98 columns with an 8px cell that is 1.225, and no plausible cell metric or row count brings it under 1 while cols ≥ 80. And a rule cannot reach back into the label before it — its left edge IS that label's span end.

**CONCLUSION, stated plainly as asked:** at the user's stated pane this does not reproduce headlessly, so the remaining candidate is real terminal compositing above the cells (kitty/sixel band images drawn over the menu rows), which no buffer-level harness can see. To settle it, the useful things from the user are: a fresh screenshot **taken against `8c9d150c` or later**, the output of `/dump-windows` on that frame, and confirmation of whether the map pane was open. The original screenshot predates the divider fix.

## 2. "UNMATCHED LINES ON THE OUTSIDE EDGES" — MEASURED, AND IT IS A RENDERER MISMATCH, NOT A POSITION ONE.

Tallied every character drawn in the frame's outer-left and outer-right columns over the frame's whole row span. At 100x71 (rows 0..69):

| | left col 0 | right col 99 |
|---|---|---|
| `┌` U+250C / `┐` U+2510 | x1 | x1 |
| `└` U+2514 / `┘` U+2518 | x1 | x1 |
| `│` U+2502 | **x6** | **x6** |
| `▄` U+2584 | x1 | x1 |
| `▀` U+2580 | — | x2 |
| space | **x61** | x59 |

Same shape at 98x69, 110x51 and 138x51. The top and bottom rules are clean single-family box drawing (`─` U+2500 x91 plus the two corners).

So one continuous vertical line is produced by **three different renderers**:

1. **box-drawing glyphs** (U+2502) — but only on the SIX menu-strip rows at the bottom, plus the four corners, because those rows are chrome TEXT strips;
2. **an uploaded image** over the ~61 story rows, which is why those cells are blank in the buffer — `decompose_chrome_strips` makes every side band (width < pane) a single `ChromeStrip::Art`, wholesale, so the `│` runs the game printed on every story row are rasterised instead of stamped;
3. **block elements** (U+2580/U+2584) leaking into the edge column at the band boundary.

This is the user's *"perhaps they are from different ascii sections"*, and it is confirmed as a GLYPH-FAMILY/renderer split — **distinct from item (B)**, the corner's floor/ceil-vs-round COLUMN mismatch. Fixing (B) would not repair this and vice versa, exactly as the coordinator suspected. Note family 3 is an artefact of the halfblocks picker the harness uses; in the user's Ghostty the ~61 rows are a true kitty image, so on their screen it is family 1 (font `│` at the terminal's own weight) against family 2 (a bitmap of the game's `│` scaled by a non-integer factor). Those cannot match in weight, colour or antialiasing however well they line up.

FIX DIRECTION, deliberately not taken: the frame's own border COLUMN in a Menu-plan flank is TEXT the game printed, not artwork, and could be stamped as glyphs like the corners and menu rows already are — making all ~69 rows one family. `flank_divider_extension` already locates that column in native pixels. It is NOT cheap: the same function serves Zork Zero's and Shogun's flanks, where the border genuinely IS artwork and a glyph would be wrong, so it needs a measured "is there a chrome text run at this native column on this row" test plus corpus proof. That is a quest of its own, not a rider on this one.
