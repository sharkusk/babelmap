## The defect

`engine.paint_surface()` -> `state.v6_paint` (`crates/app/src/main.rs:617`). The surface is allocated at the STORY WINDOW's size but the fills recorded into it are in SCREEN coordinates, so anything past the window's extent is silently dropped.

Measured on Shogun r322: the paint surface is **548x368** while the game's status erase is at native **(46, 0) 548x32** — reaching to x=594. Everything past 548 is clipped, so the fill never reaches the right flank at native 590.

## How it was found, which is the useful part

The SQ-0948 lane fixed the declared page (`fill_window_pages`) and cured the **right-hand** block only. The left one persisted, and the reason is this clipping: `blit_paint_ground` (`erase_window`, SQ-0706) is a second layer carrying the same ground, and its surface cannot represent the part of the fill that lands outside the story window. One symptom, one side each, two layers — which cost that lane a round.

So this is not hypothetical and it is not confined to Shogun: ANY v6 game whose `erase_window` fill extends past the story window's box loses the overhang. It happens to be invisible today wherever nothing downstream reads those pixels.

## Where it sits

Outside `render/` — this is the surface's allocation and indexing, not the ring that consumes it. That is why the lane left it: fixing it would have widened a render diff into the engine boundary. It wants its own lane.

The obvious fix (allocate at screen size) has a cost worth measuring first: the surface is per-frame state and a v6 screen is 640x400 RGBA, so it is not free. An alternative is to keep the window-sized allocation and record an offset, but note the fills are already in screen coordinates — so the mismatch is that the ALLOCATION is in window space while everything written to it is in screen space, and making them agree in either direction is a real choice.

Documented in `4a0f6926`'s commit message and in `crates/app/tests/suites/v6_status_window_edges.rs`'s comments, so a reader who trips on it again finds the explanation before the symptom.
