--- note 2026-08-19T02:25:13Z ---

Root cause: `/MAC/SOUND/S<n>` payloads are **offset binary** (silence at 0x80); AmigaDOS `sound/*.dat` payloads are **signed** (silence at 0x00). The ten-byte header is identical on both and records nothing about it — the flags word takes 0x0032/0x0132 on the Macintosh and 0x003C/0x013C/0x0032/0x0132 on the Amiga, so it cannot discriminate. `InfocomSound::parse` read every payload as signed, so the whole Macintosh corpus was reflected about silence.

Why it sounded the way it did: reflecting about silence turns a quiet tail into full-scale noise. Measured over Sherlock's fifteen effects, read as signed the Macintosh RMS is 2-5x the Amiga's and the mean absolute sample-to-sample step 3-8x — "very distorted and crunchy". Nothing about the duration changed; the sounds only seemed to run long because the fade-out played at full level instead of dying away.

How the sign was settled (not by ear, and not by inspection):
- Sherlock effect 8 is the same master on both discs at full rate. `mac ^ 0x80 == amiga` with **0 differing bytes in 25,820**.
- Those Amiga bytes are in turn byte-identical to `stories/Sherlock.blb`'s SSND payload on 14 of 15 effects (13 is a differently-trimmed take), and an 8-bit AIFF is signed by definition.
  So the chain is Macintosh -> Amiga -> a third party's rendition, with no link resting on judgement.
- Statistically, across all fifteen: converted, the Macintosh mean lands within 2 counts of zero and the RMS within 2% of the Amiga's.

Also measured, and worth keeping: `/MAC/SOUND` is NOT simply the Amiga corpus in another sign convention. Effects 7, 10, 11-13 and 14 are a **half-rate decimation** on the Mac disc (half the frames at half the stated rate, so same duration and pitch, coarser); 3,4,5,6,9,15,16 agree to within 0.3-0.6% of their bytes; 17 differs substantially (a different take). Only 8 is exact.

Fix: `blorb::infocom_sound::Encoding {Signed, OffsetBinary}`, taken by `InfocomSound::parse` and supplied by `from_volume` from the entry classification the layout already gives it (AmigaIndex -> Signed, MacIndex/MacSample -> OffsetBinary). `samples` keeps its documented "signed 8-bit" invariant, converted on the way in, so nothing downstream changes.

Tests: `native_disk_sound::the_macintosh_payload_is_offset_binary` (real media; asserts the effect-8 identity by differing-byte COUNT rather than by comparing 25,820 bytes in an assertion message, plus |mean| < 3 on all fifteen), and two unit cases in blorb pinning that the two encodings differ by exactly the sign bit and that the Amiga path is untouched. Falsified: with the conversion stubbed out the real-media case fails on the effect-8 identity.

Not a defect: the user also noted 11/12/13 sound alike on the Amiga. That is authentic — the Amiga pitch files are unison pairs on every file of both games, so all three heartbeats play at 18430 Hz. Only the Mac bends 13 (9215 -> 17396).

Method note: `hdiutil attach -readonly` on the treasures ISOs is the quickest way to eyeball `/MAC/SOUND` headers directly; the side-by-side statistics (mean, RMS, mean absolute first difference, per-interpretation) are what made the encoding obvious in one pass, well before any hypothesis about the header.
