CORRECTION 2026-08-09 — the "missing background paint" reframing is WRONG. The user's original report was right. Their theme background is **dark grey, not black**, and the room-description rows render black anyway. So nothing is missing: we ARE painting a black background there, and that black is what covers the artwork.

Combined with the dump in the note above, the mechanism is now specific:

- `ring: plan extend, ring clipped at row 19 (art opaque down to native y=258)` — win1 is 640x260, so the illustration is OPAQUE essentially to the bottom of the window, including the rows where the room description prints.
- Bands were uploaded for rows 1-13 and rows 17-18, but **not** for rows 14-16.
- Rows 14-16 are therefore emitted as ordinary terminal cells, and those cells carry the text runs' own (black) background.

So the room description is punching a cell-shaped hole through opaque artwork and filling it with its background. That is exactly "the text bg overwrites the graphics".

FIX DIRECTION (verify before building): where text is painted over art that is opaque beneath it, the text belongs IN the image band — rasterised into the picture — rather than emitted as cells in a band gap. The band split at rows 14-16 is the defect, not the background colour. Whatever decides where a band stops and cells resume is the code to look at; `ring: plan extend` and the `art opaque down to native y=258` figure are printed by that same logic, so it already knows the art is opaque there.

This also disposes of the "is it kitty-only?" question the quest title still carries: if the text is rasterised into the band, it composites the same way on every backend, and no per-terminal transparency mechanism is needed. Retitle when the fix lands.
