REFRAMED by the user 2026-08-09, and this is probably not a transparency bug at all. Their revised reading:

> "in splatterlight the room description has a black background (matching the background of the row of circles). I was thinking this was part of the graphic, but maybe we are missing a background paint?"

So in Spatterlight the room-description area is a BLACK PAINTED BAND, continuous with the dotted divider row beneath it — not the terminal background showing through, and not part of the illustration bitmap. The original hypothesis (text cells painting opaquely over artwork, needing a per-backend transparent path, possibly kitty-only) may be exactly backwards: we may simply be failing to paint a background the game asked for.

WHY THIS IS HARD TO SEE, and what to do about it: the user's theme background is black, so a missing black paint and a correctly painted black band look identical in their screenshot. Anyone working this MUST measure rather than eyeball — set a non-black theme background, or dump the cell backgrounds directly, so the two cases separate. A render test here must pin both `honor_game_colours` modes for the same reason: if the band is the game's own explicit colour, declining game colours is exactly where the difference shows up (compare SQ-0716, where scopa's felt turned out to be drawing rather than preference).

FIRST MEASUREMENT: what does mysterious01 actually emit around the room-description rows — an `erase_window` fill, an explicit background colour on the text runs, or nothing at all? That answers whether there is a paint to miss. Note SQ-0721 has just changed when an explicit-background row floods its window (a window is a bar only when its runs reach both its edges), and SQ-0719-era work aside, this band is precisely the shape that rule governs.
