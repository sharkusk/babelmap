## SCOPE CAVEAT — see SQ-0976's note; OSC 4 is riskier here than for Infocom art

Programming the terminal palette to "the game's sixteen colours" is an Infocom-shaped idea. Generic Glulx graphics windows carry arbitrary PNG/JPEG — photographs, full-colour art — where a 16-entry reservation would be badly wrong and would visibly posterise.

So the indexing must be conditional on the frame, measured not assumed: count distinct colours in the composite, use indexed SGR only when they fit the reserved range, and fall back to truecolour otherwise. Note also that even an all-Infocom frame can exceed 16 once own-palette pictures, adaptive pictures reading the Current Palette, and chrome text from the period-look table are composited together.

Two further constraints on the OSC 4 half, independent of the above:
- It mutates the terminal's palette globally, so it needs a reserved range (16-31, say) and restoration on exit — including on an abnormal exit, which is where this kind of thing usually goes wrong.
- Not every terminal honours it. Needs a capability probe, and a truecolour fallback that is always correct.
