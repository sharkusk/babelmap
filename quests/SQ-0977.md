## SCOPE CAVEAT — see SQ-0976's note; OSC 4 is riskier here than for Infocom art

Programming the terminal palette to "the game's sixteen colours" is an Infocom-shaped idea. Generic Glulx graphics windows carry arbitrary PNG/JPEG — photographs, full-colour art — where a 16-entry reservation would be badly wrong and would visibly posterise.

So the indexing must be conditional on the frame, measured not assumed: count distinct colours in the composite, use indexed SGR only when they fit the reserved range, and fall back to truecolour otherwise. Note also that even an all-Infocom frame can exceed 16 once own-palette pictures, adaptive pictures reading the Current Palette, and chrome text from the period-look table are composited together.

Two further constraints on the OSC 4 half, independent of the above:
- It mutates the terminal's palette globally, so it needs a reserved range (16-31, say) and restoration on exit — including on an abnormal exit, which is where this kind of thing usually goes wrong.
- Not every terminal honours it. Needs a capability probe, and a truecolour fallback that is always correct.

--- note 2026-08-22T02:05:01Z ---

## OUTCOME: do not build it. Measured, rejected, and the finding is pinned.

Closing as done rather than discarded because work shipped — two tests and a doc section — but nothing was implemented, and nothing should be.

**The 16-colour premise is true of the ARTWORK and false of the WIRE.** Across 709 pictures in zork0/shogun/journey/arthur.mg1, the richest carries 15 colours; sixteen is structural, since a decoded `Picture` is one index per pixel through a `[Rgb; 16]`.

But `Halfblocks::encode` resolves one sample per column and two per row, and `resize_directional` picks Triangle on any axis that shrinks — which invents intermediate colours. Zork Zero picture 41, 15 colours on its 640x400 canvas:

  pane 117x64  -> grid 117x74   ->    665 colours
  pane 200x60  -> grid 192x120  ->    614
  pane 458x144 -> grid 458x288  ->  1,165
  pane 640x200 -> grid 640x400  ->     15   <- only here

The grid stops shrinking only when the pane has as many COLUMNS as the canvas has pixels across. Shogun's richest illustration reaches 15,539 distinct colours on the wire.

**OSC 4 is therefore dead on arrival** — a reserved range would need thousands of entries, not 16 or even 240. No probe to design, nothing mutating the terminal palette, nothing to restore on exit, and the Windows/`install_termination_handlers` question never arises.

**Indexed SGR alone is rejected on quality**: it would cut ~30% of the stream (154 KB of a 489 KB frame) but costs a mean Euclidean RGB error of 21-26 with only 5-6% of emissions exact (worst 61-67) — visible posterisation on exactly the dithered art where the saving concentrates. A conditional "index only exact cube entries" saves ~1.3%; "index only when the whole frame fits" never fires. Both are dead code.

Measured before-state, for anyone revisiting: at 458x144 cells a full repaint is 489,334 bytes of which 81.7% is SGR, 13,017 SGR for 65,952 cells (ratatui's coalescing IS working — one per five cells; the art simply has short runs), 1,419 distinct colours. Half-blocks captures contain zero APC graphics commands, so SQ-0991's `o=z` is genuinely a separate path.

Pinned in `crates/app/tests/suites/v6_halfblocks_colour_depth.rs` and a `docs/features/v6-graphics.md` section, so nobody re-derives it. Falsified by forcing `resize_directional` to Nearest, which makes the case fail with "14 distinct colours from 15 in the artwork".

Follow-ups filed separately rather than dropped.
