--- note 2026-08-17T15:12:18Z ---

## The score is NOT rasterised — that part is working

`stories/shogun-r322-s890706.z6`, two turns in. At every pane size measured the status line comes back as **`strip:text(8 runs)`** and is drawn with glyphs. So the user's first question ("why is the score rastered in the first place?") has the answer "it isn't" — what they can see is a few native pixels of it appearing a *second* time, inside the flank band beside it.

## The defect: the flank's source over-reaches the art

MEASURED with `ring_scout --story stories/shogun-r322-s890706.z6 --turns 2 --size WxH --bands`, against the frame art's true extent (art-only canvas, `build_graphics_canvas`):

**The frame art runs native columns 0..46.** The status window (win1) is `548x32@(46,0)` and its first run, `"Erasmus"`, starts at native **x=49**.

| pane | flank source width | native px past the art (46) |
|---|---|---|
| 80x24 | `48x432` | 2 |
| 98x37 | `52x514` | **6** |
| 129x60 | `50x670` | 4 |
| 169x47 | `49x400` | 3 |

The flank's native extent is obtained by inverting its **cell** box, which is rounded OUTWARD so the band covers the art's last pixel. That rounding is up to a whole terminal cell, and a cell is `8 / s` native px — so the over-reach is scale-dependent, which is exactly why the sliver's thickness changes as the window is resized.

Those extra columns are then cut from the **chrome canvas**, not the art canvas. `flank_tiled_source(… &canvas, &gfx)` classifies from `gfx` and cuts from `canvas` (the deliberate "classify from what was painted, cut from what is presented" pairing), and `build_chrome_canvas` rasterises chrome glyphs into it. Verified pixel by pixel across the boundary at both status rows: art through x=45, then from x=46 on the chrome canvas is opaque where the art canvas is empty — the status window's page and the leading edge of its glyphs. Magnified by the flank's own factor, that is the visible sliver.

So one glyph column is drawn **twice**: crisply, as part of the text strip, and again as resampled pixels inside the flank band immediately to its left.

## Fix direction

Clamp the flank band's native source to where the art actually ends rather than to the inverted cell box. `flank_art_columns` already measures that boundary; the band keeps its cell box (the last cell is tiled/stretched anyway), it just must not *read* pixels past the art. Note the two obvious alternatives are worse:

- Clearing the runs' pixels from the canvas inside the flank's columns leaves a transparent hole over real frame art — Shogun's border is a slab spanning the full height, so those rows do carry art.
- Cutting the flank from `gfx` instead of `canvas` loses the pages and painted ground that pairing exists to supply.

Falsify on `129x60` and `98x37` (thickest sliver), both `honor_game_colours` modes, and check Zork Zero and Arthur do not lose their flanks' outer pixel to an off-by-one in the other direction.

## Separate, and probably not a defect

At some sizes the status becomes a **full-width** text strip and the flanks start below it — `98x37` gives `strip:text 98x2 at (0,0)` with flanks at `(0,2)`, where `129x60` gives `strip:text 109x2 at (10,0)` between full-height flanks. That is the bridging/flood rule deciding whether the row's runs fit in the gap, and it is what the user described as "stretches across the window at smaller sizes". Worth confirming with them whether the full-width form is wanted before touching it; it is not the sliver.

--- note 2026-08-17T15:37:28Z ---

**User ruling (2026-08-17): the full-width status form is CORRECT and PREFERRED.** When the score's runs do not fit in the gap between the flanks, the row spanning the whole pane with the flanks starting below it is the wanted behaviour — not a fallback to be tightened, clipped or boxed in. Do not touch the bridging/flood rule as part of this quest.

The scope of SQ-0902 is therefore only the **drawn glyphs**: the few native columns of rasterised status text the flank band picks up because its source over-reaches the frame art. Everything above the "Separate, and probably not a defect" heading in the previous note stands; that final section is settled and closed.
