--- note 2026-08-14T04:38:57Z ---

Widened by SQ-0836's findings: the segmented Apple II story container is NOT only a 5.25-inch problem. `Arthur Quest 4 Excalibur.2mg` and `Journey.2mg` are 800K ProDOS volumes that mount perfectly and contain NO whole story file — the Apple II press splits each game across `ARTHUR.1/ARTHUR.D1`…`.D5` and `JOURNEY.D1`…, and none of those parts begins with a Z-header. (A raw byte scan finds a header partway INSIDE `.D1` — at offset 37 970 for Arthur, 4 690 for Journey — which is what fooled an earlier probe of mine into reporting them as releases r63/s890622 and r77/s890616. Neither is a whole story; treat both numbers as retracted.)

So this quest is really "read Infocom's segmented Apple II story container", and it has two carriers rather than one: the `.D1`–`.D5` files inside a ProDOS volume, and the filesystem-less 140K `.dsk` sets (Shogun s1–s5, Zork Zero 1–4) where the same splitting happens with no directory at all. Solving the container solves both; solving only the `.dsk` sector layout leaves the IIgs v6 games unreachable.

Both `.2mg` disks currently mount, list their files and correctly report no game, so nothing is broken — the games are simply not yet readable.

--- note 2026-08-14T06:19:18Z ---

The IIgs PICTURE format is an open question this quest will answer, because the art has nowhere else to live.

ProDOS listings of the two IIgs v6 disks show no separate picture archive at all:

  Journey.2mg   JOURNEY.D1 55296, .D2 124928, .D3 136192, .D4 137216
                plus PRODOS, INFOCOM (22528, the interpreter), INFOCOM.SYSTEM, INFODOS, JOURNEY.DOCS
  Arthur .2mg   ARTHUR.D1 86016, .D2 116736, .D3 126464, .D4 137728, .D5 108544
                in per-volume directories ARTHUR.1/ … ARTHUR.5/, plus the same interpreter trio

So the story AND the art are both inside the `.D*` segments. Journey's four segments total ~453 KB, far more than the story alone, so the bulk of it is artwork.

`blorb::infocom_pics` currently decodes exactly two flavours: `AmigaMac` (big-endian, Huffman + RLE + per-line XOR) and `Pc` (little-endian GIF-style LZW, no XOR). There is no Apple flavour. Whether the IIgs needs a third is UNKNOWN and must not be assumed either way. A hypothesis worth testing but not believing: the IIgs super-hi-res mode is 320x200, 16 colours per line from a 4096-entry palette, which is close enough to the Amiga's that Infocom may have reused the `AmigaMac` codec — plausible, and entirely unverified, since nobody has seen a byte of IIgs picture data through this reader.

`InfocomPics::parse` already picks its flavour by measurement rather than by signature (neither flavour carries one, and the two headers occupy the same bytes), so the natural first test once a `.D*` segment is readable is simply to hand its picture region to the existing parser and see whether either flavour claims it.
