CONFIRMED BY THE USER as Zork Zero's hint menu, with Shogun behaving the same way. That is a much stronger clue than "two titles are broken", because SQ-0934 already measured these two screens against each other and established they are ONE screen:

  - identical ring artwork on every medium of both titles (top 78%, flanks 70%, middle 0%)
  - the same four header strings at the same y (1, 17, 33) — same InvisiClues subsystem
  - the ONLY difference is the middle rect that carries the topic list:
        Zork Zero PC r393    GRID 468x322 @ 86,78
        Zork Zero Amiga r366 GRID 464x320 @ 88,80
        Shogun PC r322       GRID 500x330 @ 70,70
        Zork Zero Mac r296   full-screen GRID 640x400 (buffer KEPT, not withdrawn)

So two DIFFERENT middle rects produce the SAME symptom. That points at the transform rather than at any per-title constant or at which cells the click map claims — and it matches the user's own two-pane-width observation, where the defect changes shape between 190x60 and 50x60 rather than simply being offset.

This is the continuation of SQ-0934's last open item ("mouse clicks land on raster locations, not the glyph cells"), which landed as SQ-0938 (555b08e8, "a click resolves the way the pixel under it was drawn") and was confirmed working in general.

START HERE — the coverage gap that let it through: the packed-region cases in `crates/app/tests/suites/v6_mouse_zork0.rs` (`a_click_in_a_packed_region_selects_the_row_and_column_under_the_pointer`, `a_row_only_packed_region_leaves_the_column_proportional`) are SYNTHETIC. They are built from Zork Zero's real hint-screen numbers but never drive the actual menu, so nothing clicks a real topic and asserts the right one is selected. A case that boots the hint screen, clicks a named topic at several pane widths and checks the selection is the first thing to write — and the Mac press is a useful fourth specimen, since it keeps its buffer and reaches the ring by a different route.

Both pane widths the user reported should be pinned (190x60 and 50x60), plus the Shogun press, since a defect that changes shape with width is exactly what a single-width case would miss.
