THE PROMPT offers three outcomes: **peel**, **ignore**, **mark for later**.

- "Ignore" MUST be remembered per-seam/region and persisted. A prompt that reappears on the

- "Mark for later" needs somewhere to live and a way to act on it later, or it is just a
  slower "ignore".

DESIGN COMPLETE — settled with the user 2026-08-12. Implement this; do not redesign it.

SHAPE: detect and SUGGEST, never act (see the previous note for why auto-peel was rejected).
A prompt offers three outcomes: PEEL / IGNORE / MARK FOR LATER.

"Mark for later" is NOT a list, a badge or a new command. It simply RE-ARMS the prompt for the
next crossing of that seam. The three options are then a gradient over one mechanism —
peel now / ask me again next time I come through / never ask about this seam — and the only
state is a per-seam flag with three values, persisted with the map as layer metadata already is.

TWO INDEPENDENT TRIGGERS, one prompt, one memory:

1. STRUCTURAL
   - GATE: the region on the far side of the seam is reachable ONLY through portal edges
     (in/out/up/down) — no in-plane path back. A cellar reached only by `down` qualifies; a
     balcony you can also walk to does not.
   - FLOOR: the region is >= 4 rooms. (User's number. It exists only to suppress trivial
     prompts.) Compute the region with `peel_region`, which already answers "the region on the
     far side of a portal seam" — so the suggestion cannot disagree with what the peel would do.
   - TIMING: prompt when the player steps back ACROSS the seam, not mid-exploration. You finish
     the cellar, climb the stairs, and THEN the map asks. If the player never comes back, they
     are never asked — deliberately conservative. There is NO "hasn't grown in K turns" fallback.

2. SEMANTIC (the user's addition)
   - Room name matches /\bmaze\b/i — word boundary, case-insensitive, so "Amazement" and
     "Amazed" do not trip it.
   - Fires on the TRANSITION into a matching room from a NON-matching one — one prompt per
     entrance, silence inside. Zork I's maze is ~15 rooms all named "Maze"; prompting in each is
     the nag failure this design exists to avoid. The transition is also exactly the seam a bare
     `peel-layer` cuts ("the passage you just walked in through", SQ-0552), so trigger and
     action agree by construction.
   - BYPASSES the floor and the timing rule. Those exist because structural evidence is weak at
     first contact; the name IS the evidence and is available immediately.

ACCEPTING A NAME-TRIGGERED PEEL ALSO SETS THE MAZE FLAG. A peel from the structural trigger
does NOT. (User's call: the player confirmed it is a maze by accepting a prompt that said so.)

MEMORY: an ignored seam stays ignored; a layer that was merged back is never re-suggested.
Persisted with the map.

PROMPT MECHANICS: the project's standing dialog conventions — shared chrome, Tab/Shift-Tab to
move focus, Enter activates, Esc cancels (Space is widget-reserved). Must not steal focus
mid-turn.

STILL OPEN (ask the user, do not assume): whether a layer already flagged as a maze is exempt
from the STRUCTURAL trigger. It was proposed and not contradicted, but not explicitly confirmed.

PREREQUISITE: SQ-0826 removes the old asymmetry-based detector (`tangles`/`offer_maze_layer`,
SQ-0666), which is the same detect-and-suggest idea on evidence that fires late, never, or on
an ordinary ring of rooms. Do not resurrect it, and do not build on `matrix::tangles`.

* **The auto maze→matrix view switch is REMOVED, the matrix view is KEPT.** The user's words: "the auto-switch maze to matrix display has not been working properly, so we can probably remove that feature all together", then, on being asked: "no, i want to keep the maze/matrix view. only remove the auto detection that isn't working."

## The manual half: peel and merge anchored on a SELECTED room

The user's original complaint, verbatim: "the issue i've had with peeling is that the peel direction is not clear, especially since we need to specify the peel based on the edge direction. and if the edge is one way into the room we want to peel there is no direction to select."

**The diagnosis is that today's `peel-layer [direction]` is anchored on the room you are STANDING IN.** So to peel the area you are in you must name the direction pointing back OUT of it, which is inverted from how the player is thinking; and if you arrived through a one-way passage there is no direction to name at all — which is exactly why `peel_across(.., true)` had to exist as a separate path for the bare command (SQ-0552). Re-anchoring on a SELECTED room dissolves all of it. `state.selected_room` already exists and already carries `RenameRoom`, `EditNotes` and the room dock.

**Which side of the seam gets peeled: the side of the SELECTED ROOM. Always.** This is not a new rule — `peel_at_edge(from, dir)` already peels `from`'s OWN region. The room is the anchor; the edge is only the seam. No preview, no flip, no disambiguation UI.

**How the seam is chosen, in three tiers — no edge selection required:**

1. **Portal boundary.** `peel_region(selected)` walks out across compass edges and stops at portal edges (no grid offset — up/down/in/out). That is a floor, and it needs no input beyond which room was picked. The common case.
2. **The unique inbound bridge.** When the region is not portal-bounded, `planar_region` walks straight through and `peel_region` refuses `WholeLayer`. Then look at the bridges INCIDENT TO THE SELECTED ROOM, oriented as arrivals — passages from outside INTO it. Exactly one → cut it, and SAY what was cut. One-way inbound edges are included, which is precisely the case that previously had no direction to name.
3. **Pick-list.** More than one inbound bridge → offer them by name ("north, to Forest Path").

The user's ruling, verbatim: "auto-pick when unambiguous, ask only when not. and the pick-list should be based on paths coming into the selected room."

**Why edge selection is NOT needed, and this is the load-bearing observation:** a peel can only ever cut a BRIDGE. `peel_at_edge` refuses `NotASeam` when "its two ends stay connected by some other route, so cutting it separates nothing." So the set of legally cuttable edges is exactly the bridges out of the region — computable, usually tiny, often one. Pointing at an edge cannot select anything outside that set, so edge selection would buy ergonomics and nothing else. Deferred.

**Consequence of anchoring on the entrance:** selecting a room in the MIDDLE of a maze offers no inbound bridge, so it falls back to the portal-boundary peel or refuses. The user was told this explicitly and did not object. The anchor is the entrance room, not any room in the region.

## MERGE GENERALIZES; there is no `move-room`

The user spotted the redundancy: "we have the command merge already, so we should standardize on move or merge, probably don't need both."

The real gap found on inspection is one of GRANULARITY, not naming: `peel-layer` is region-granular (takes a region out, makes a layer) while `merge-layer [target]` is layer-granular (puts a WHOLE layer into its parent or a named one). They are therefore not inverses — there is no way to say "put just this room into that layer", and a peel that grabbed one room too many can only be fixed by merging the whole layer back and re-peeling.

So: `merge-layer` keeps its current meaning, AND merge also accepts the selected room or its region — "put this into that layer". Peel and merge then become true inverses at both granularities, and no new verb is introduced. Grid-position nudging was offered as an alternative meaning for `move-room` and was NOT chosen; it is not part of this quest.

## Prompt UI and persistence

* **Shared-chrome modal**, chosen over a non-blocking map-pane offer and over the room dock. Standing dialog conventions apply: Tab/Shift-Tab moves focus, Enter activates, Esc cancels, Space stays widget-reserved. It must not steal focus mid-turn.
* **Deferrals AND refusals persist** into the archive with the map. A restored game must not re-ask about a seam already declined. Consistent with "persist the recipe": these are player DECISIONS, not derived state, so they cannot be recomputed and must be carried.
