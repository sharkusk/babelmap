DESIGN RULING, user 2026-08-11: **"if a game draws a border, the artwork should not overlap it."**

That answers this quest's load-bearing question directly, and rules out the alternative. The two candidates recorded above were:
  (a) exclude the border columns from the picture's allocated span — a LAYOUT change;
  (b) tolerate art in the border column and stamp the run's character over it — a COMPOSITING change.
**Take (a).** The picture's allocated span must stop short of any column the game draws its border in, so column 1 (and the matching column on the right) is never inside the art rect to begin with. Do not composite a rule on top of artwork to paper over an overlap.

This is a general rule, not a Journey patch: it applies wherever a game draws a border and artwork shares the region. Expect it to interact with the flank/picture-box geometry (`menu_flank_panel`, and whatever computes the picture window's allocated columns), not with `flank_border_extension`'s content test — the content test was doing the right thing by refusing to stamp a glyph where the canvas is inked; the bug is that the canvas should never have been inked there.

Fault 2 (row 2 unclaimed on the right) is plausibly the same overlap seen vertically — the strip's rows being handed to the art instead of to the frame. Check whether the ruling fixes it as a side effect before writing separate logic for it.

Fault 3 (the `║` double-weight rule at 121x36 against a single-weight top, where 117x64 gives `│`) is NOT addressed by this ruling and remains open on its own terms. Answer it by measurement first: dump the game's paint run at that native column under BOTH sizes. If the game prints `║`, the top border being single is the defect; if we choose it, the run→glyph mapping is. Do not guess between those.

--- note 2026-08-11T23:39:45Z ---

CORRECTION to fault 3 as I filed it, settled by measurement in the fixing lane (commit 3251fd2b).

I recorded: "The right rule is drawn DOUBLE-WEIGHT against a single-weight top — `║` (U+2551) at 121x36 where 117x64 gives `│`. The chosen glyph is width-dependent." I framed that as the most alarming of the four faults and asked whether the game or our run→glyph mapping picked it.

**It is neither. The `║` and `█` are babelmap's own transcript SCROLLBAR.** `║` is ratatui's `symbols::scrollbar::DOUBLE_VERTICAL.track` and `█` its `thumb` (verified in `ratatui-core-0.1.2/src/symbols/scrollbar.rs`; `Scrollbar::default()` selects that set for a vertical orientation in `ratatui-widgets-0.3.2/src/scrollbar.rs:185`). `transcript.rs:2401` draws it at `area.right() - 1`, the story viewport's last column — one column left of the frame's rule. It is present at 121x36 because the intro overflows a 33-row pane, and absent at 117x64 because it fits.

The game itself prints plain `│`: all three runs dumped as `run x=1 y=145 style=00 "│"` at native x 1, 257 and 633. No `║` is emitted anywhere.

So there was never a width-dependent border glyph. I compared a real rule in one capture against a scrollbar in another and filed the difference as a defect. The lesson for anyone reading a glyph grid: the story viewport's last column can carry the scrollbar, and it sits immediately beside the frame's rule — check `transcript.rs`'s scrollbar before concluding a border changed character.

Fault 3 therefore required no fix. Faults 1, 2 and 4 were real and are fixed by the plan-gating change.

The cell grid accounts for only TWO of the three lines — column 0 is babelmap's pane border, column 1 is the glyph rule this quest's fix stamps. **The third line is inside the artwork bitmap**, which is why it does not appear in the glyph grid at all.

TWO DETAILS FROM THE USER THAT CONFIRM IT AND ARE WORTH KEEPING:
  - **"slightly thicker than our standard border"** — a native pixel column scaled up is fatter than a one-cell font glyph. That is the signature of a rasterised rule sitting beside a stamped one, and it distinguishes this from any glyph-placement error.
  - **"not for all widths"** — at small scale factors the border's native pixel column quantises into the SAME cell as our stamped rule and hides inside it; at 236 columns the scale is large enough that it claims a cell of its own. So the defect is scale-dependent and will be invisible at the pane sizes most tests use. Any test must sweep to a wide pane.

THE FIX: exclude the border's native columns from the artwork's SOURCE CROP, not merely from its destination rect. Trimming the destination alone shifts the border pixels inward — which is exactly what we now see, the game's own rule appearing BESIDE ours instead of UNDER it. The canvas handed to the placement must simply not contain those native columns.

Check the right-hand side and the top/bottom edges at the same time: the same source crop presumably carries the picture's own right rule and its horizontal edges, and the report only mentions the left because that is where two lines are adjacent enough to notice.

ACCEPTANCE, added to this quest's existing criteria: at 236x68 (and across a sweep including deliberately WIDE panes, where the scale factor is largest) exactly ONE rule stands between the pane border and the artwork, and no scaled rule appears inside the art's first columns.
