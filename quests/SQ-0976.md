## SCOPE CAVEAT — the "sixteen colours" reasoning is Infocom-only

This quest was filed off an analysis of Infocom v6 artwork, where every picture really is <=16 colours (verified: every palette type in blorb::infocom_pics is [Rgb; 16], and 640x400 Amiga hires is 4 bitplanes, so 16 is the hardware ceiling there too — 32/EHB/HAM all need lores).

**That does not generalise.** The user notes lanthorn also has generic Glulx graphics, where images are arbitrary PNG/JPEG — photographs and full-colour art with no palette at all. Any indexed-transmission work must therefore:

- COUNT the frame's distinct colours rather than assume a bound, and fall back to truecolour/RGB when it exceeds what the target can index.
- Not assume a fixed 16-entry reservation is enough. Even within Infocom, a COMPOSITED frame can exceed 16: a picture with has_own_palette() carries its own table, adaptive pictures read the Current Palette, and chrome text is drawn from the period-look table (the Amiga's #074BA1 page, say) which need not be in any picture's sixteen.

The `o=z` half of this quest is unaffected and is the safe, universal win: zlib compression helps flat palette art enormously and costs nothing on a photograph beyond the compression attempt. Do that first and independently of any indexing work.
